# Five fictional temporal-transport candidates: tests, advantages and limitations

9 September 2026. All five ideas are retained. None currently constitutes a completed, stable nonexpanding matter–light–gravity theory. The comparison uses explicit restricted versions where possible, with published or previously extracted real data; unformulated interactions are marked untested rather than assigned a fit score.

## Scope and evidence

The new calculations reanalyze 1,504 DES event-average duration estimates, released per-band width estimates and 43 COBE/FIRAS monopole spectral channels. These are previously inspected, processed data. DES widths inherit the reference-curve construction and its dilation assumptions, and the FIRAS calculation uses released residuals with diagonal errors rather than a full calibration/covariance likelihood. The study is an exploratory diagnostic, not independent confirmation of a new theory.

The separate-geometry assessment also uses the preceding analytical derivation and existing Pantheon+ brightness results, identified as previous results rather than rerun evidence for a new action. No new raw atom–cavity records, full nonlinear action, stability solution, CMB angular fit or angular-size likelihood is available in this comparison.

## 1. Path-dependent time memory

The simplest explicit version assigns frequency evolution d ln(nu)/d ell = −kappa but leaves a fixed-speed arrival map t_o = t_e + R/c. This produces the desired frequency redshift and preserves a straight ray, but the arrival-interval derivative is dt_o/dt_e = 1. Consequently it predicts no stretching of the entire transient. Path memory that records only how far a photon traveled does not by itself change that result.

Fitting the derived DES event widths to w = (1+z)^b gives b = 1.00288 with formal statistical error 0.00483. The normalized-width RMSE is 0.5870 for no dilation and 0.1531 for unit-exponent dilation. Their diagonal chi-squared values are 30,117.4 and 2,166.8 for 1,504 points with no fitted exponent in those two fixed predictions. These are conditional derived-data diagnostics; the formal error and chi-squared should not be read as a full systematic or independent exclusion significance. The unit-stretch model is not a perfect statistical fit either.

**Advantages:** the exponential frequency law is simple; a physical memory variable could keep track of cumulative history; no density screening is logically required. **Disadvantages:** the stationary fixed-speed version misses whole-signal stretching, and the status of memory inside cavities or atomic interactions is undefined. A stationary linear filter can delay and broaden signals but cannot universally rescale their entire time axis for arbitrary emission times.

**Outcome:** the tested frequency-only version is inadequate under the adopted DES interpretation. A richer version must supply an emission-time-dependent travel history or other explicit mechanism with dt_o/dt_e = 1+z. That requirement moves it toward the time-evolving coherent propagation model below. The class of all history-dependent theories is not excluded.

## 2. Coherent exchange with an evolving timing field

The explicit propagation proxy is H_wave = c0 |p|/n(t), with n(t) = 1 + gamma t and present normalization n_o = 1. Spatial momentum is conserved in the homogeneous medium, while frequency changes as 1/n. Differentiating the fixed-path ray integral produces dt_o/dt_e = n_o/n_e. Frequency and pulse stretching therefore agree and reproduce exp(kappa R) when gamma = c0 kappa.

This proxy predicts the unit-exponent DES result above: normalized-width RMSE 0.1531, versus 0.5870 for frequency loss without envelope stretching. It is compatible with the measured duration scaling within the published processing assumptions. If an initially thermal occupation distribution evolves adiabatically with uniformly rescaled frequencies, its thermal form can be preserved. Neither result identifies a microscopic interaction or a thermal origin.

**Advantages:** it links two distinct observables using one ray calculation, and can retain coherent images and narrow spectral features at geometric-optics order. **Disadvantages:** the earlier explicit electromagnetic constitutive completion changes the leading hyperfine response when it protects optical binding. The previous clock comparison remains a failure for that completion. Matching tensor propagation still requires a nonlinear gravitational action, and energy transferred to the field changes its dynamics unless backreaction is controlled.

A simpler alternative that multiplies an otherwise standard wave kinetic term by a time-dependent normalization does not automatically accomplish the same thing. At leading geometric-optics order it changes amplitude transport while leaving the static-metric phase cone unchanged. A frequency and envelope effect must be derived, not inferred from a changing coefficient alone.

**Outcome:** this is the best-developed transport candidate among the five, but only at the level of propagation kinematics. The data support retaining its whole-signal behavior as a target, not declaring its failed atomic completion repaired.

## 3. Wavelength- or scale-dependent response

An explicit observational proxy lets the pulse-duration exponent vary by optical band: w_band = (1+z)^b_band. It tests one consequence of chromatic temporal transport; it is not a direct measurement of wavelength-dependent spectroscopic redshift and does not identify the interaction that protects atoms.

| Optical band | Valid width estimates | Fitted exponent b | Formal statistical uncertainty |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| g | 361 | 1.0212 | 0.0272 |
| r | 1,380 | 1.0043 | 0.0083 |
| i | 1,465 | 0.9876 | 0.0077 |
| z | 1,381 | 0.9973 | 0.0082 |

The per-band samples differ, so subtracting those estimates without accounting for objects would be misleading. A paired comparison restricted to the same 342 objects observed in g and i gives b_g − b_i = 0.0181. A 500-resample paired event bootstrap gives a descriptive 95% interval of −0.0070 to +0.0496. This interval includes zero. It accounts for resampling events, not the unavailable shared-reference covariance or all color-dependent systematics. The g–i contrast is a chosen diagnostic, not an exhaustive blind chromaticity search.

**Advantages:** scale dependence offers additional ways to separate microscopic binding and long-distance propagation; the response could be constrained experimentally across frequencies and cavity sizes. **Disadvantages:** these optical data show no compelling need for different duration exponents. A frequency cutoff alone cannot distinguish cavity light from freely traveling light at the same frequency. Extending one response across optical photons and much lower-frequency gravitational waves also requires explicit calculation rather than extrapolation from these four bands.

**Outcome:** retain as a possible microscopic ingredient, with nearly achromatic optical envelope transport as a constraint. This test neither confirms atomic protection nor sets a universal bound on frequency dependence from radio to gravitational waves.

## 4. Distinct matter and propagation geometries

The universal conformal subfamily has already been explicitly derived. With g_m = B(phi)^2 q and all matter minimally coupled to g_m, local material clock ratios retain their ordinary behavior and photon/tensor characteristics coincide. But material-measured homogeneous redshift is A_o/A_e, where A = B a is the material scale. Keeping A fixed makes that redshift zero. Choosing B to restore the exponential makes physical separations expand relative to material rulers.

The genuinely nonconformal alternative remains unspecified as a successful interacting theory. Different temporal and spatial couplings may evade the conformal calculation, but a freely chosen wave metric is not itself an atomic Hamiltonian, a gravitational action or a stable background. Therefore no honest new likelihood score can yet be assigned to this alternative.

The earlier fixed-material curvature law improved the 960-supernova statistic from 898.5 for flat geometry to 873.0 for fitted negative curvature. It remains a kinematic brightness result, not support for this unconstructed action. Its paired angular-distance prediction differs from the metric coasting interpretation: at z=1 their angular distances are 2.92 and 1.46 Gpc respectively, although both give luminosity distance 5.84 Gpc under the same adopted calibration. No new angular-size data have been fitted here.

**Advantages:** this is a direct mathematical route beyond the conformal obstruction; it can link propagation, lensing and angular distance once an action is supplied. **Disadvantages:** atoms and traveling light use the same electromagnetic field, making consistent separation difficult. Modified tensor dynamics, extra modes, stability and energy exchange must be checked together.

**Outcome:** retain as an action-building framework. The conformal version has a demonstrated redshift obstruction; the genuinely nonconformal version is open, not data-validated. This comparison does not claim to have tested every possible two-geometry action.

## 5. A thermal reservoir and recirculating radiation

Two separate propositions can be tested: whether redshifted thermal components can simply be mixed into the observed CMB, and whether an explicit equilibrium interaction has a Planck distribution as its stationary state.

For the first, fit the FIRAS spectrum with equal weights of two thermal components at T exp(+delta) and T exp(−delta). For every fixed delta, fit the central T and residual Galactic-template amplitude. The total spectrum is reconstructed from the exact 2.725 K reference blackbody plus the released residual column, avoiding rounding in the tabulated reconstructed total intensity.

| Trial spectrum | Diagonal chi-squared, 43 channels and two fitted parameters |
|---|---:|
| One temperature | 45.02 |
| Two temperatures about ±0.1% apart from the center | 45.03 |
| Two temperatures about ±1% apart from the center | 235.81 |
| Two temperatures about ±10% apart from the center | 1,737,972 |

The broader mixtures substantially worsen this restricted fit even with the foreground-template coefficient free. A conditional Delta chi-squared = 3.841 threshold occurs at delta = 0.00379, about ±0.38% in temperature. This is a descriptive profile threshold relative to the zero-separation fit, not an exact boundary-calibrated confidence limit or a bound on every possible source mixture. Full calibration and interchannel covariance are not included. The fitted reference temperature near 2.72500054 K is not a new absolute CMB temperature measurement.

Thus circulating radiation with different source temperatures and path histories does not automatically yield a single thermal spectrum. This example quantifies the difficulty without claiming that all mixtures are impossible. A mechanism that brings the radiation to equilibrium remains a different hypothesis.

For that mechanism, an explicit single-boson-mode collision term is

df/dt = Gamma_plus (1+f) − Gamma_minus f.

If Gamma_minus/Gamma_plus = exp[h nu/(k_B T_bath)], the stationary occupation is f = 1/(exp[h nu/(k_B T_bath)]−1). A numerical check confirms this algebra. It is a detailed-balance construction, not an inference of the rates or reservoir from data. It predicts a Planck form only after the reservoir temperature and the appropriate rate ratio have been specified.

**Advantages:** a thermal reservoir supplies an actual way to reshape radiation rather than only shifting it. It could operate in a closed, nonexpanding universe without assuming a Big Bang. **Disadvantages:** this construction does not derive 2.725 K, the energy supply, the equilibration rate or the CMB angular patterns. Continuing redshift and stellar injection are additional driving terms; detailed balance alone does not ensure that the complete driven system remains at one constant temperature. Absorption, emission or scattering must also preserve the observed transparency and transient signals where required.

**Outcome:** retain a genuine thermalization mechanism as a CMB-origin candidate. Do not count an equilibrium blackbody fit as evidence for its microscopic cause. Passive recirculation and broad thermal mixing are substantially less promising than a specified energy-exchange mechanism in the tested examples.

## Comparative decision

The most useful transport branch is coherent time-dependent propagation, because it derives spectral and envelope stretching together. A nonconformal matter–wave interaction is a possible framework for completing that branch but is not yet a successful theory. Scale dependence remains a possible ingredient constrained by approximately common optical-band duration behavior. Pure stationary path-dependent frequency loss misses envelope stretching. A thermal reservoir is a separate candidate for the CMB and must eventually be coupled consistently to the same transport field.

These ideas are not mutually exclusive competing finished models, so ranking their chi-squared values across different datasets would be meaningless. The next common milestone remains one microscopic interaction and background solution. None of the fits here solves exact atomic protection, shared nonlinear tensor propagation and stable physical nonexpansion simultaneously. All five branches remain documented with their specific limitations.
