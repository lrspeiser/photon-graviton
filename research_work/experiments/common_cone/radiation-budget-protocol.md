# RB-1: can outgoing field energy supply the missing outer force?

Declared 20 September 2026 before numerical evaluation, during SR-1 enlarged-domain runs. This is an analytic necessary-condition screen of the SR-1 Hamiltonian, not a new observational fit or a radiation simulation.

In the weak-field, eta=lambda=omega=0, high-frequency massless-wave limit, <Pi^2>=<|grad F|^2>=e in c=1 units. Differentiating SR-1 field energy with respect to phi gives g[(1+3s)<Pi^2>/2+(1-s)<|grad F|^2>/2] = g(1+s)e. Since slow matter couples as g rho, normalization to measured weak static G gives

laplacian Phi = 4 pi G [rho + (1+s) u/c^2],

where Phi=c^2 U and u is physical wave energy density. Neglect vector shifts, mass, coherent near-field stresses, higher-order feedback and source evolution for this necessary-condition calculation. The coefficient 1+s is derived here; it is not a tunable additional gravitational charge.

For isotropic outward luminosity L, u=L/(4 pi r^2 c). Between emission radius r0 and a finite outward front R, M_active(<r)=(1+s)L(r-r0)/c^3. Thus v_wave^2=G(1+s)L/c^3*(1-r0/r), which approaches a flat profile. However its required luminosity is L=v_flat^2 c^3/[G(1+s)]. A source mass M can supply at most epsilon M c^2; lifetime is epsilon(1+s) G M/(v_flat^2 c). Even epsilon=1 exhausts all rest energy and is not physically acceptable for a persistent galaxy.

For a stored rather than escaping population with the same positive wave-energy source coefficient, E_required=v_flat^2 c^2 (R-r0)/[(1+s)G]. Trapping reduces replenishment luminosity but does not remove this energy requirement. This relation applies to the specified weak wave branch only, not arbitrary nonlinear constitutive mechanisms. Slower massive waves cannot be modeled merely by replacing c: their dispersion and active-source coefficient must be derived again.

Evaluate 54 combinations of s={0,1}, v={100,200,300} km/s, illustrative source masses M={2e40,2e41,2e42} kg and available rest-energy fractions epsilon={1,1e-3,1e-6}. Target lifetime 1e10 Julian years (365.25 days/year is a unit convention). Compute required L, lifetime and required active coefficient for that lifetime. Do not treat these masses/speeds as observed objects. Reject the persistent freely escaping-wave explanation in a fixture if its energy budget cannot last the target time; numerical consistency passing is separate.

Independently integrate 36 shell-energy/mass profiles: s x v x M is unnecessary for profiles, so use s={0,1}, v={100,200,300} km/s, R={6e20,6e21} m and r/R={.1,.5,1}, with r0=R/100. Compare quadrature-derived force against the analytic finite-front expression and numerical shell energy against the stored-energy formula (relative errors <1e-10). No infinite-energy stationary background is allowed. Preserve every result and record all assumptions.

Constants: G=6.67430e-11 m^3 kg^-1 s^-2 from NIST https://www.nist.gov/how-do-you-measure-it/how-do-you-measure-strength-gravity ; exact c=299792458 m/s from BIPM https://www.bipm.org/en/si-base-units/metre . Mass and speed fixtures are declared inputs, not data. Spherical flux conservation and Poisson integration are established mathematics; only the active coefficient is derived for our specified Hamiltonian.
