# Dense distance inputs: what can be reused

2026-09-10. Source/feature audit only; no fitted redshift, new inferred distances or withheld outcome evaluation. The prior goal turn made progress by testing brightness-distance consistency on all existing targets. The redshift calculator remains available; this catalog work concerns a separate test of environmental dependence.

The TF paper distinguishes measured photometry and adjusted linewidths from its final distances. Its cosmological factor f appears in a derived Hubble-parameter calculation; its presence alone does not prove every photometric distance was obtained by redshift inversion. However, the final selection rejects some Hubble-parameter outliers, and parts of the merged compilation receive redshift-dependent adjustments. These dependencies must be tracked before the final catalog can serve as independent environmental evidence. [TF source, Sections II and IV–V](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.00733)

The SDSS Fundamental Plane reduction includes a 4 log(1+z) surface-brightness correction, an evolution correction and model-dependent distance/selection inference. That reduction cannot simply be relabeled as our static model, whose conditional bolometric dimming differs. This does not invalidate the measured angular sizes, fluxes or dispersions, or imply that the empirical Fundamental Plane intrinsically requires dark matter. [FP source, Sections 2 and 5](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/515/1/953/6611706)

We retrieved the TF measurement-stage table through a query that omitted velocity and distance-outcome columns. It contains 10,737 unique PGC identifiers, with i-band photometry present for 7,502 and W1 photometry for 5,478. The sentinel -100 is counted as missing, not a physical magnitude; presence is not full photometric quality certification. Metadata describe Wmx as adjusted linewidth and icmag as corrected magnitude; these must not be called unprocessed observations. [CDS metadata](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/ftp/J/ApJ/902/145/ReadMe)

Exact PGC matching finds 109 overlaps with the earlier selected distance-indicator tracers: 75 TRGB, 24 Cepheid, nine SBF and one maser. Of these, 74 have i-band and 97 have W1 photometry. They are possible calibration inputs, not new independent holdouts; history, shared calibration, associations and corrections remain pending. The restricted earlier tracer list excluded the 164 exact redshift targets, so these are not counts of every available potential calibrator.

The next environmental task can start from these measured features and explicit corrections, rather than importing a velocity-reconstructed void field. It still requires a selection model, a stated physical environment rule and distance uncertainty. None of these prerequisites removes the existing exponential redshift calculator or establishes the cause behind it.
