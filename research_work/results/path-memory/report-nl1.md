# NL-1: a response not proportional to its source, the root spectrum
20 September 2026. Archive `nl1-results.json`, runtime 305 s; suite job `nl1_checks.py`. Inputs: CL-2 stage 2's caches and archive, the X-COP extract and release correlations, CR-2's lens interface under the static geometry.

**The question, in plain language.** Stage 2 certified that no field proportional to the ordinary matter, blurred by any positive combination of widths, describes the galaxies. The classic remedy makes the extra pull grow as the square root of the ordinary pull; its local form, PM-1's law, passes the galaxies by a hair on the consistent source and falls ten times short on the clusters. NL-1 asks whether the same root response applied to the written field at every width, one amplitude set for every system, keeps the galaxies while bringing the clusters and lenses inside their acceptances.

**Outcome.** b: the galaxies are described (20.78 km/s) but no joint minimiser brings the clusters (term 0.69) and lenses (kinematics term 1.78, Einstein term 9.10) inside. At the joint minimiser the cluster term is inside its acceptance and the galaxy and lens terms are not; taken alone, the galaxies pass and the clusters score within 6% of the release reference.

## The clusters
Under the release's SZ correlations and the corrected boundary fit, one root spectrum for twelve clusters scores **12.32 per point** against the release reference's 11.64 (ratio 1.06); PM-1's local law scores 113.1 and the baryons alone 248.1; stage 2's linear spectrum scored 14.01. Active members: 0.158 kpc, 63.1 kpc, 251 kpc, 10 Mpc. The clusters alone are described (within a factor two of the reference). The first complete run, with the widths below 1 kpc excluded on the 1500-point grid, scored 13.92 with active members 39.8 kpc, 63.1 kpc, 251 kpc, 398 kpc, 10 Mpc; the rebuilt columns admit the narrowest width, and the clusters take it.

That member is the point of the experiment. The clusters put an amplitude of s = 221.6 on the 0.158 kpc member; the galaxy optimum puts 0.261 on the same member, a factor 849 in the root amplitude (the square of that in a). At the clusters' radii, 18 kpc and beyond, that column is the root of the written field's own force at a width far below the data, which follows the gradient of the gas density rather than the enclosed mass: its cosine with the local root column is 0.79 to 0.92 across the 12 clusters (computed from the cluster cache at report time, a diagnostic outside the archive), so it is not a local member in disguise. The transfer test shows the same gap from the other side: the cluster optimum on the galaxies gives 942 km/s.

## The lenses and the joint solve
Lenses alone (static geometry, well coupling, the three-dimensional bend of each root basis force): kinematics chi-square 223.8 with a worst Einstein residual of 11.1% (128 and 3% allowed); active members 0.398 kpc, 0.631 kpc, 6.31 kpc, 39.8 kpc, 63.1 kpc.

The balanced joint objective, each block divided by its acceptance so that one means "at the threshold": galaxies 40.020, clusters 0.694, lens kinematics 1.776, Einstein 9.099; the joint minimiser gives galaxies 138.54 km/s, clusters 16.15 per point, lenses 227.3 and 15.9%, with active members local, 0.398 kpc, 0.631 kpc, 10 kpc, 63.1 kpc, 100 kpc, 251 kpc. Described at the joint minimiser: galaxies no, clusters yes, lenses no. The joint solve is not a compromise between three nearly satisfied blocks: the galaxy optimum transferred to the lenses costs a kinematics term of 154, so the minimiser gives up the galaxies to keep the lens terms small, and the lenses still fail.

Transfer without retuning: the galaxy optimum on the clusters gives 220.9 per point, on the lenses 19701.5 and 63.1%, on the Milky Way 21.5 / 12.8 km/s (baryons alone 52.6 / 62.3); the cluster optimum on the galaxies 941.5 / 1047.5 / 977.3 km/s, on the lenses 892851.3 and 530.7%, on the Milky Way 1375.9 / 1340.9; the joint minimiser on the Milky Way 216.6 / 203.1.

## What is not shown
The root response is a fitted law on exposed data, with no energy budget, formation or propagation behind it; its local member has the prior art of the Bekenstein toy function and the nonlocal members are proposals. The galaxy sources are stage 2's razor-thin reconstruction; the cluster columns below 1 kpc carry stage 2's recorded convergence of 5.7e-3 under grid doubling; the lens kinematics are mean-field with constant anisotropy; no held-out system.

## Next decision
The galaxies and the clusters are each described by a root spectrum, and the lenses by none; the two amplitude sets differ by a factor 849 on the member that carries the cluster response, so no one set serves all three. The cluster problem of root laws persists under nonlocal sourcing, now with the trade-off measured. The next questions are what the lenses need beyond a root response, and what could set the amplitude of the narrowest member by the system it acts in, an energy budget being the natural candidate.
