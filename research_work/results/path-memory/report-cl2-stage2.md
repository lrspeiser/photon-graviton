## S2-2 and S2-3: one family everywhere, one source per galaxy
The family is the 25 widths from 0.158 kpc to 10 Mpc, five per decade, on every block. The clusters' columns are all resolved on a 6000-point grid (stage 1 used 1500 and zeroed widths below 10 kpc): against 12000 points the columns change by at most 3.5e-05 (A1795) and 3.7e-05 (A2319) for widths of 1 kpc and more, 6.3e-04 and 5.7e-03 below 1 kpc; the 0.158 and 0.251 kpc columns, whose convergence is not required, contribute at most 2.0e-04 of the Newtonian pressure at the largest amplitude any fit assigns them.

Each galaxy's Newtonian force is now computed from the same reconstructed components as its written force: razor-thin stellar and gas disks by the exact ring kernel with the uniform-sheet subtraction, and the spherical bulge. Against the SPARC tables (which carry the survey's own three-dimensional disk models and measured gas profiles), the reconstructed force is 1.027 of the tabulated one at the median point, 0.84 to 1.31 between the 10th and 90th percentiles, 0.38 to 13.5 at the extremes (2 tabulated points are exactly zero). The baryons alone score 53.7 km/s (reconstructed) and 52.6 km/s (tabulated) on the training set. This discrepancy is a property of the source reconstruction, recorded as the protocol asked; both forces are carried through.

## What is not shown
The source reconstruction for the galaxies is razor-thin with an exponential gas disk, cruder than the survey's own models, which the recorded force ratio quantifies; the certified minimum is a property of the family on these sources. No held-out system; exposed data throughout. The cluster comparison still rests on hydrostatic pressures with the release's modelling inside them, and the reference is the release's pipeline consistency number, not a floor. The lens kinematics are mean-field with constant anisotropy. No formation, energy budget or propagation at the inferred strengths.
