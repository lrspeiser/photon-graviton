# Lensing and motion from the depth-based injection profile

This is a synthetic, conditional shape prediction, not a fit to cluster data. The preceding goal turn made progress by adding the depth-capture calculation and committing it to main. This continuation projects the same calculated profile into lensing and motion observables. All six research objectives remain open.

## Assumptions and provenance

Retain the optional kappa=chi*(-Phi)^4 capture postulate and fixed ordinary Plummer well from report.md. Assume stored density is proportional to the instantaneous injection q(r), with no subsequent migration, and use equal weak gravitational potentials with negligible stress. Those storage and gravity-response assumptions are not established by the transport calculation. Spherical projection, circular-speed and weak-lensing identities below are known mathematics, not novel formula claims. The conditional profile generated by this capture law is the model-specific input.

For total deposit mass M_d, scale a and sigma=4 pi integral x^2 q(x) dx, take rho_d(r)=M_d q(r/a)/(a^3 sigma). Define the dimensionless projection S(b)=2 integral_0^infinity q(sqrt(b^2+z^2)) dz. Physical surface density is M_d S(b)/(a^2 sigma). The observable weak-shear shape is DeltaSigma=mean enclosed Sigma minus local Sigma. Actual shear also requires source/lens distance geometry through Sigma_crit; reduced shear additionally depends on convergence. Neither is fitted here.

## Central projection result

For this smooth nonzero-central profile,

    S(b)=S(0)+B b^2+o(b^2),
    B=integral_0^infinity [q(r)-q(0)]/r^2 dr,
    DeltaS(b)=-B b^2/2+o(b^2).

The expression for B follows from differentiating the line-of-sight projection and integration by parts with the central value subtracted. Using integral q/r^2 without that subtraction, as applicable to the earlier zero-central-density candidate, would incorrectly diverge here.

A projected central-curvature transition occurs at A=2.7453264176. This is distinct from the three-dimensional curvature transition A=1.8332747454. Line-of-sight averaging therefore hides the initial central hollowing. Negative tangential shear means a radial image-stretching tendency of this component; it does not mean repulsive gravity or negative mass. Other hollow distributions can share this behavior, so it is not a unique signature of companions.

Ordinary Plummer matter contributes positive central tangential shear. With the same scale a and a frozen deposit shape, total central shear changes sign only above M_d/M_b=124.305 at A=3 or 105.865 at A=10. These large ratios invalidate neglect of deposit backreaction; they are algebraic shape comparisons, not self-consistent physical thresholds or exclusions. At A=10 the deposit shear is negative at b=0.1a and a, but positive at 3a and 10a.

## Linked motion and bending

The same density gives v_d^2(r)=G M_3d(<r)/r and physical bending angle hat_alpha(b)=4G M_2d(<b)/(c^2 b). At r=b,

    hat_alpha/(v_d^2/c^2)=4 M_2d(<b)/M_3d(<b).

This removes the common unknown mass normalization. At b=a this ratio is 6.518 at A=0.1, 7.235 at A=1, 9.383 at A=3 and 25.692 at A=10. It provides a joint shape test once the baryonic contribution, geometry, storage support and capture parameters are specified. Circular speed is not a prediction of every star's velocity in a pressure-supported cluster or bulge.

## Verification and next requirements

lensing.py and lensing-results.json preserve five strengths and four radii each. Projected aperture masses computed from spherical caps agree with independent image-plane integration to better than 1e-7 in the stated surface-density scaling. Central curvature agrees with finite differences to better than 1.3e-6 in its stated scaling. Doubling angular nodes from 128 to 256 shifts the transition by 1.1e-12. An independent analytic optically thin projection, S/A=pi/[2(1+b^2)^(3/2)], agrees to 5.9e-7 at A=1e-6, consistent with finite absorption corrections.

Weak capture removes the earlier acceleration-squared candidate's unavoidable central projected dip in this isolated example. This is a reason to retain the depth candidate for further testing, not evidence that nature selects it. Required next steps include physical support/redistribution of deposits, a consistent potential reference and self-gravity, followed by calibrated joint motion/lensing data.
