On the recovered 164-group data, direct conversion and the explicit expansion benchmarks have almost identical redshift errors. The paired descriptive difference intervals span zero. This supports continuing to test the branch, but does not prove equivalence: all rows were already exposed, environmental and distance-calibration provenance remain incomplete, the bootstrap conditions on fitted predictions, and no equivalence margin was prespecified.

The objective is now comparable prediction with calibrated uncertainty, using a fixed common model and tolerances specified before the next independent evaluation. A claim to replace the expansion explanation more broadly requires comparable performance on the other observations in that claim, including transient timing, brightness and spectra. Strict numerical improvement is not required.

## Has the general idea been proposed before?

Yes. Fritz Zwicky proposed a gravitational interaction explanation for light's redshift in 1929 in [On the red shift of spectral lines through interstellar space](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/f0px7-y7k36). Such nonexpansion energy-loss ideas are generally called tired-light models. The familiar fractional-loss equation dE/ds=-alpha E and its exponential redshift solution are not new physical mathematics. Zwicky's later [Remarks on the Redshift from Nebulae](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.48.802) also investigated spectral-line width, showing that preservation of spectra was already a central issue.

Photon-graviton conversion also appears in theoretical literature, including the Gertsenshtein mechanism in external electromagnetic fields. [Hwang and Noh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04150) analyze that conversion. This is not automatically the process needed here: converting a photon into another excitation is different from continuously lowering the frequency of a surviving photon, and it does not by itself provide a stable galactic deposit.

The current proposal combines partial photon energy transfer, lossless companion propagation and capture into extended bound configurations. That is the specific combination to investigate. No comprehensive prior-art search establishing its uniqueness has been completed. Do not call the whole idea unprecedented or treat related theoretical conversion as an observed process.

## Why an energy-loss trend is not already a complete solution

1. **Carrier frequency versus event duration.** The minimal stationary fixed-speed model makes photons redder but leaves the separation between two emitted flashes unchanged. In contrast, [DES measurements of 1504 supernovae](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05050) find light-curve widths scaling approximately as 1+z, under the study's source-population and analysis assumptions. The observation must be explained, even if expansion is not its cause. This is a problem for the stated non-time-dilating closure, not a theorem that every conceivable interaction must fail.
2. **Energy transfer versus spectral and image preservation.** Some scattering mechanisms also deflect light or broaden spectral lines. These consequences depend on the actual interaction; they are not unavoidable merely from the phrase tired light. A proposed coherent or forward transfer must calculate them rather than assume them absent.
3. **Brightness and distance consistency.** The loss law changes flux. A brightness-derived distance therefore cannot silently be treated as a geometric distance under every theory. The new report explicitly evaluates both conventions as conditional comparisons; a full source-calibration forward model is still required.
4. **Transport versus a persistent halo.** Carrying the missing energy does not show that it is captured, remains extended, or generates the required lensing and stellar motions. The bound-mode interaction, support and gravitational response must supply this link. Energy-supply quantity remains deferred at the user's request.

These are calculable requirements, not an appeal to consensus. An alternative that meets them with comparable predictive performance is within the user's goal. Our next mechanism task is to obtain frequency, timing, angular and energy behavior from one physical interaction, rather than treating the successful redshift trend as completion of the theory.

## Why supernova timing is not only a nearby-object question

Some supernovae are nearby with small redshift, but they are not the whole timing sample. The cited DES study uses measured redshifts roughly 0.1 to 1.2. This statement uses observed spectral shifts; it does not require assigning expansion-derived distances as facts in our universe.

At our illustrative 100-million-light-year conversion prediction, 1+z=1.0076605, so a putative full-event stretch would be only 0.766%. That would indeed be hard to isolate from the intrinsic diversity of supernovae in a single event. At measured z=0.5 the factor is 1.5, and at z=1 it is 2. A ten-day source interval would correspond to fifteen or twenty observed days under that relation. These numbers are illustrative interval mappings, not claims that every supernova has the same ten-day duration.

In the minimal static, constant-speed transfer model, two photons emitted ten days apart traverse the same fixed path and each takes D/c to arrive. Their arrival separation is therefore still ten days, even if both photons lose half their energy. This standard travel-time subtraction under the model's assumptions distinguishes carrier redshift from the temporal envelope of a transient. A physical conversion process might modify the envelope too, but our current energy-only equation has not derived such behavior.

Supernova timing tests compare populations and matched spectral/bandpass behavior, with source diversity and systematic errors; they do not directly watch a remote source's local clock. The DES analysis reports a fitted duration exponent b=1.003 +/- 0.005 statistical +/- 0.010 systematic in a dependence on (1+z)^b, conditional on its analysis assumptions. The relevant obligation is to reproduce that measured trend or provide a quantitatively supported alternative account. It cannot be set aside on the grounds that all supernovae are too close to be redshifted.
