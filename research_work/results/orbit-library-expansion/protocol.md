# Training-only coverage expansion

This prospective rule is written before integrating or scoring the new paths. It improves the numerical representation of a candidate potential; it is not a new gravity fit or independent validation.

Keep the existing ordinary-matter field, full-bar empirical extra potential, solar frame, bar parameters, quality screen and training/validation/test assignments fixed. Use only the existing training posterior means and archived 72-orbit coverage audit. Do not change distances or discard distance-disagreement flags.

Select four additional launches in each of the nine existing radius/absolute-height cells (36 total). Eligible launches pass the existing positional screen and were not among the original 72. A target is initially uncovered when the old library has no saved state within both 0.5 kpc and 50 km/s. Within each cell, greedily choose the eligible star whose initial state covers the largest number of still-uncovered eligible targets under those same two distances, then remove those targets from the selection objective. Break ties by increasing integer source ID. If marginal gain reaches zero, stop that cell rather than claim a useful launch. This prefers missing populated states over isolated extreme velocities; it does not select by gravity residual. Selection counts are not post-integration results.

Integrate each new launch through 0.25 kpc/(km/s), approximately 244 Myr, with 501 saved times. Retain the previous DOP853 tolerance sequence 2e-9, 2e-11, 2e-13 and require successive paths to agree within 1e-4 kpc and 0.01 km/s, plus Jacobi drift/220^2 below 1e-5. Compare fine/finer extra forces at every saved position with the unchanged 1% threshold. Save failed paths and attempts; use only passing paths in the expanded numerical library.

Compare the old and expanded libraries on the identical eligible training sample excluding every old and newly chosen launch star, and exclude t=0 states. Report 0.25/0.5/1-kpc and 25/50/100-km/s resolutions, signed-height and radius cells, and half-time sampling. Also report a late-half-only library sensitivity to avoid equating proximity soon after launch with a representative long-lived population. These windows are finite coverage diagnostics, not proof of stationarity. Verify exact neighbor-search results against brute force on fixed indices and verify that adding paths cannot reduce coverage on the same target set.

No orbit weights, selected likelihood, gravity parameters, holdout scores or photon interpretation are fitted. A later comparison must give ordinary-matter and companion candidates equivalent library flexibility, propagate measurement/selection and mass-model uncertainties, and establish adequate orbit occupation sampling before scoring gravity.
