# Hypothetical companion-powered black-hole environments

User-requested fictional branch, 13 September 2026. Reinterpret observed nuclear electromagnetic emission as companion-powered. This does not identify it as observed Hawking radiation. Emission can occur outside the horizon; escape from inside a conventional event horizon is not assumed.

Known energy bookkeeping gives L_gamma=f_gamma P_release, dot(E_res)=P_capture-P_release, and a processed mass-equivalent rate L_gamma/(f_gamma c²). The proposed ingredient is the identification of the release channel with companions. f_gamma is the fraction of processed energy escaping in photons, distinct from the one-third retention factor. Other output channels must be counted. If unused energy remains stored, only actual outgoing energy is subtracted from the reservoir.

Take 10^29 W as an approximate reference ceiling for Sagittarius A*, following the bolometric <=10^36 erg/s scale in [EHT Paper I](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ac6674). At f_gamma=1 it corresponds to 1.77e-11 solar masses/year of energy, and at 0.1 to 1.77e-10. This is a conditional reservoir-throughput estimate, not a measured black-hole mass-loss rate.

The representative M87 core luminosity 2.7e35 W corresponds to 4.77e-5 solar masses/year at f_gamma=1. It is from a ~32 pc aperture and published distance 16.4 Mpc in [Prieto et al. 2016](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/457/4/3801/2588956), not emission isolated at the event horizon. Its jet-dominated, direction-dependent emission means the isotropic-equivalent luminosity is only a benchmark for intrinsic power. Mechanical jet power is excluded, and adding it changes the total budget. These historical source states are not a simultaneous present-day sample.

There are two different branches. A settling-release branch consumes available mechanical binding energy while retaining constituent rest mass at Newtonian order. A reservoir-evaporation branch consumes stored rest energy and changes the earlier permanence assumption unless replenished. Nuclear brightness alone cannot distinguish them or measure the capture efficiency, inventory and release time separately.

The prior Milky Way settling budget Q=4.527e50 J, if entirely available to this nuclear channel, would take 1.43e14 years to radiate at the adopted Sgr A* reference power. This is a budget-to-power ratio, not a universe-age claim or a predicted duration. Over an illustrative billion years, that power emits only 6.97e-6 of Q. Equivalently, if all Q were released in a billion years, only that fraction could emerge through this currently faint nuclear photon channel at its reference power. The rest would require other locations/channels or a different activity history. Delivering all Galactic settling energy to the nucleus has not been demonstrated.

Results are in `nuclear-release-results.json`; all quantities follow directly from the formulas above with c=299792458 m/s, solar mass=1.98847e30 kg and year=31557600 s. No fit to spectra, variability, imaging or a second galaxy's predicted companion inventory is performed. A predictive next test would freeze a release law from independently calculated inventory and local conditions, then compare brightness, spectra and variation across sources. Assigning observed luminosity as input reconstructs an energy requirement; it does not predict the observation.
