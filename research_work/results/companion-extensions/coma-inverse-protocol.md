# Coma inverse distribution: finite positive profiles

Declared before execution. Use the six already reconstructed Kubo figure bins and plotted errors, including the negative outer shear. Radius ratios only; no physical distance, critical density or dark-halo mass is imported. This is an exposed-bin weak-shear inverse diagnostic, not a full reduced-shear likelihood or independent validation.

Represent total gravitating density by a nonnegative sum of spherical Plummer components. This gives finite positive mass, analytic surface density and excess surface density. Ordinary gas/stars have not been separated by this fit, so the result is a total-gravity envelope, not uniquely the companion mass. Existing Coma data lack matched baryonic/source normalization needed to subtract it. Preserve that limitation explicitly.

For component scale a in first-observed-radius units and central convergence w>=0, kappa(R)=w*a^4/(R^2+a^2)^2, gamma(R)=w*a^2*R^2/(R^2+a^2)^2 and M/(pi Sigma_crit R0^2)=w*a^2. Use 25 log-spaced scales from 0.1 R0 to 10,30,100 times the final measured radius as three outer-extent sensitivities. These are kernel scales, not sharp boundaries. Also find min/max total dimensionless mass subject to every predicted shear lying within two plotted errors and the same positivity/kappa constraints. These are conservative coordinate-wise feasibility bands, not confidence regions, probabilities or mass measurements. If no feasible set exists, report that rather than enlarging bands. Solve corresponding min/max enclosed mass at the last measured radius. Do not select the outer scale by best fit or turn its finite-grid maximum into a physical upper mass bound.

Check LP feasibility, minimum residual constraints, positivity, and Plummer projection against direct numerical line-of-sight integration. Retain all three sensitivities and profile weights.
