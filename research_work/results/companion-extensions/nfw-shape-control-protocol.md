# NFW shape controls at fixed added-component bending

13 September 2026. Written before the runs.

Compare the recorded companion density with standard NFW density rho_s/[x(1+x)^2], x=r/rs. Test three fixed scale choices rs/a_capture=0.3, 1 and 3. For each galaxy/population/scale, choose rho_s so the added component produces exactly the same bending at the catalogue lens radius as the recorded companion profile. This is a controlled shape comparison, not an independently calibrated dark-matter halo prediction, a free best-fitting NFW model, or a cosmological concentration-mass relation.

Retain the conditional nonexpanding optical geometry and all measured data. At each stellar gradient the same catalogue angle fixes stellar mass. Since added bending at that angle is matched, its stellar-mass calibration at a given gradient is unchanged. Fit the same four stellar nuisance parameters to all motion bins, with the same bounds and central/sampled orbital conditions. No new local halo parameter is optimized on motions within an individual scale run. Evaluating several scale choices is additional model selection and must be reported.

Use the previous 27 starts plus the companion variable-radius optimum, evaluated with the substituted NFW force. Require the optimized score no worse than this feasible configuration. Preserve explicit reference evaluations to reproduce old companion objectives. Check analytic NFW enclosed mass against the density integral, convergence of normalization quadrature, lens-angle matching and full covariance decomposition. Use the ideal untruncated NFW profile for the shape diagnostic; it has logarithmically divergent total mass, so do not call it a finite companion inventory or formation model. Its finite-radius force, relative potential and deflection are well defined.

Report every scale and system, not just the best case. Separate the four stellar fit parameters, three scanned scale choices and reference-inherited normalization. The result can identify useful redistribution at fixed lensing strength, but cannot establish superiority to dark matter in general or a radiation origin for the density.
