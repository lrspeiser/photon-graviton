# First new finding: nearby sources under no-loss transport

With the same 814-source catalog, 26 targets, present-day luminosities and 10-billion-year benchmark, two explicit no-loss companion arrival assumptions increase median external flux by 0.2073% and 0.6237%. The median combined local-plus-external required/supplied ratio changes from 685403784 to 685125082 or 684566111.

This is a controlled sensitivity calculation, not a complete no-loss universe. The earlier shared-loss flux is independently reproduced to relative 1e-12 before changing its energy factor. Photon conversion fraction, geometry, local supply and duration remain fixed. The two arrival laws bracket two declared scenarios; they are not a proof that every allowed propagation law lies between them.

For x=kappa*d, the original per-source flux factor is x*exp(-2x). No companion energy loss gives fraction 1-exp(-x). With arrival stretching S=exp(x), the new factor is (1-exp(-x))*exp(-x); with no companion arrival stretch, it is 1-exp(-x). The first gain is (exp(x)-1)/x, approaching 1+x/2 for small x. Nearby sources therefore gain little from removing secondary loss because their paths are short under this coefficient.

This keeps the no-loss concept open. Larger/distant source populations, a much older consistent radiation history, different capture, an initial reservoir with a source, or a derived stronger gravitational response remain possible branches. None has been selected or silently normalized to the observed rotation curves.

The pure full-conversion bound at constant current luminosity would require a median 5.43e+13 years for each target's own luminosity, even with perfect conversion and retention. This is not a viable-age estimate; it is a conditional requirement and does not include extra external emitters or altered gravity.

A further audit note: the archived variable external_no_stretch_over_stretched compares x to x*exp(-2x), removing both attenuation factors. Its label should not be used as a clean test of arrival stretching alone. Original files were preserved; the new scenarios use explicitly named energy and arrival assumptions instead.
