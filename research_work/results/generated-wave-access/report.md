# Can the generated companion waves enter a restoring region?

## Question and result

The previous environmental calculation showed that a region can resist slow changes of the companion/time field while admitting sufficiently rapid waves. That is useful only if the photon interaction actually generates those rapid waves. This calculation puts a photon packet, its receiving field and a restoring slab in one evolving Hamiltonian system. The companion pulse is generated by the interaction, rather than supplied as an independently chosen input.

**In the tested examples, almost all incoming companion energy is reflected.** For restoring frequency m=1, about 0.15-0.17% passes through by the end of the calculation. For m=3, the transmitted fraction is smaller still. This narrows the earlier favorable transmission result: fast waves can pass, but this particular finite conversion region produces little energy at those frequencies. It is a limitation of this source/environment candidate, not a rejection of every cumulative-time or companion theory.

The energy is conserved. There is no absorption channel or permanent deposit in this model. Temporary energy inside the monitored region is reported separately from both transmission and reflection.

## 1. A joint, explicitly conditional model

Use c=1 and a positive effective optical factor. The field displacement is phi, its velocity is u, and the photon packet has position X and positive canonical momentum P. The Hamiltonian is

```
H = integral K/2 [u^2 + v^2 phi_x^2 + m(x)^2 phi^2] dx + P/n_bar(X),
n_bar(X) = 1 + integral W_sigma(x-X) g(x) phi(x) dx.
```

W_sigma is a normalized Gaussian sampling kernel. The static conversion envelope g is cos^2(pi*(x-2)/4) for 0<x<4 and zero elsewhere. A photon crosses this finite interaction region and then continues freely, leaving a finite, dynamically generated companion disturbance. This spatial envelope is an optional diagnostic assumption, not a derived gravity-dependent coupling or a completed explanation of voids. It replaces the earlier spatially uniform packet coupling to let us follow a complete emitted pulse without switching an external source on and off in time.

The restoring coefficient m is nonzero only in -6<x<-4, to the left of the conversion region. Its static profile is also stipulated. No self-gravitating galaxy, material receiver or dynamical matter-density equation is supplied. In particular, neither g nor m is adopted as the final environmental law or an established means of protecting atomic clocks.

Hamilton's equations give

```
phi_t = u,
u_t = v^2 phi_xx - m(x)^2 phi + [P/(K n_bar^2)] W_sigma(x-X) g(x),
X_dot = 1/n_bar,
P_dot = (P/n_bar^2) partial_X n_bar.
```

The derivative with respect to X differentiates the sampling kernel while holding the field and spatial profiles fixed. Both the photon force and its field source come from the same Hamiltonian. Since it has no explicit time dependence, total energy is conserved. More specifically,

```
dE_photon/dt = -(P/n_bar^2) integral W_sigma g u dx,
dE_field/dt = -dE_photon/dt.
```

There is no pre-existing rolling field in these runs: phi=u=0 initially. All field energy therefore comes from the photon packet. The packet energy is a classical loading parameter, not a measured individual-photon frequency or a graviton count. The model does not establish achromatic redshift or intensity independence.

The fixed spatial profiles can exchange momentum with the field and photon, although they do no work in this static approximation. In the continuum their force on the tracked system is

```
F_profiles = (P/n_bar^2) integral W_sigma g_x phi dx
             - (K/2) integral (m^2)_x phi^2 dx.
```

A completed matter model must supply the opposite reaction and any resulting motion. The numerical check integrates the exact rate of the discrete field-plus-photon momentum, including the fixed profiles and the spatial grid. It is not a claim that an unmodeled material environment conserves momentum by itself.

## 2. Measuring actual entry, reflection and exit

The photon begins at X=-2 with energy 0.03 and moves right. The companion speed is v=0.5, field normalization K=1, and the slab lies to its left. Thus the wave traveling toward the slab takes its own path. The packet does not cross the slab or directly supply energy inside it; the conversion envelope vanishes there exactly.

Monitors at x=-3 and x=-7 bracket the slab and adjacent empty space. On either massless side, the continuum directional energy-flux magnitudes are

```
F_left  = K v/4 (u + v phi_x)^2,
F_right = K v/4 (u - v phi_x)^2.
```

At the right monitor, leftward energy is incident and rightward energy is reflected. At the left monitor, leftward energy is transmitted. The small apparent rightward component in the no-slab control measures finite-grid directional-decomposition error; it decreases with refinement and is explicitly included in the ledger. No nonnegative mode-energy fraction is substituted for the actual local flux.

The discretization uses cell-centered fields and nearest-neighbor gradient energy. Its face flux is -K v^2 times the mean neighboring u times the face gradient. The directional expressions use these same face quantities, so their signed difference equals that flux exactly. The monitored energy obeys

```
E_inside(t) = E_incident(t) - E_reflected(t) - E_transmitted(t)
             + E_incoming_from_left(t) + source_work_inside(t).
```

Inside includes the slab and the space between the monitors. It is temporary field energy, not deposited mass. The direct source-work term is exactly zero here.

The periodic computational domain is -32<x<64, evolved to time 50. The physical wavefronts do not reach its ends during the observation window; the code also checks the energy in boundary strips. The photon has left the conversion region before the reflected disturbance could return to it. Initial conditions, source location and finite observation time are part of this test, not universal propagation conditions.

For sigma=0.25 without a slab, the photon loses about 0.0008479 of its initial 0.03 energy, or 2.83%. The leftward pulse carries about 0.0002184 past the monitor, approximately 25.8% of that lost photon energy. Other field energy is accounted for by the full-domain Hamiltonian; the leftward incident pulse is not the total companion supply. These numbers are dimensionless examples and have not been scaled to a galaxy or fitted to redshift data.

The source spectrum explains the poor access. For sigma=0.25, half of the incident wave energy lies below angular frequency about 0.209, and 99% below about 0.734. About 99.953% lies below the m=1 propagation threshold and 99.999918% below m=3. The wider packet gives similar characteristic frequencies and an even smaller high-frequency tail. The finite spatial extent and evolution of the interaction set this spectrum; it was not chosen to match the receiving region.

Subthreshold waves can tunnel through a finite slab, so below-threshold energy is not synonymous with exactly zero transmission. Nevertheless, this source puts very little energy into the high-frequency band for which the previous transmission calculation was favorable.

## 4. Independent checks and finite-time limits

Twelve coupled runs cover two smoothing widths, three restoring frequencies and two spatial resolutions, with 1,536 and 3,072 cells. The maximum total-energy drift is below 6e-12 of initial photon energy. The maximum local energy-balance residual is below 4e-15 of incident energy. The exact discrete momentum-rate comparison and boundary-contamination checks also pass. These are numerical consistency checks, not astronomical validation.

Refining the grid changes the reported reflected/transmitted/remaining fractions by at most 3.0e-6 in absolute fraction and the incident energy by at most 6.1e-5 relatively. For the very small m=3 transmitted fractions, relative changes can be appreciably larger than those absolute numbers suggest; the output retains both resolutions.

An independent frequency-domain comparison Fourier transforms the incident pulse from the no-slab source run and weights its energy by the earlier continuum slab transmission coefficient. The transform retains the zero-frequency component and is zero-padded only to refine frequency integration. It does not create additional signal data or impose an input spectrum.

This comparison predicts eventual transmission of approximately 0.00171254 and 6.2513e-7 for sigma=0.25 at m=1 and 3. The time-domain values at time 50 are approximately 0.00171363 and 6.1885e-7. At m=1, their difference is smaller than the observed spatial-grid change. At m=3, some ringing energy remains inside and can still leave either side. Both smoothing widths agree within a diagnostic interval formed from the finite-window transmission, all remaining internal energy, and the observed mesh change. This is an empirical numerical error allowance, not a rigorously certified continuum bound. Raw spectral/time-domain relative differences are preserved in the output rather than hidden.

### Why temporary energy is not a permanent deposit

No absorbing or bound receiving state exists in this model. Moreover, the continuum operator -v^2 d^2/dx^2 + m(x)^2 for a nonnegative finite slab has no square-integrable localized normal mode on the open line. Its quadratic form excludes negative squared frequency. Positive squared frequency gives oscillatory exterior solutions, which cannot be square-integrable unless identically zero; zero frequency gives exterior linear solutions and likewise no nontrivial localized mode. A zero-energy constant displacement in the all-massless case is not a stored gravitational energy source.

Thus a finite pulse can produce transient penetration and ringing, but this ideal positive barrier supplies no permanent localized energy store. The finite periodic numerical box is only used before boundary returns and is not invoked as a storage mechanism. A physical capture channel, its reverse reactions and spatial support would have to be derived separately.

## 5. Consequences for the research program

The earlier result that slow-field suppression can coexist with high-frequency transparency remains correct. The new result is that the proposed source must actually populate the transparent band. For this optional source profile and field law, it largely does not. Increasing a barrier's static suppression cannot be assumed to preserve the delivery rate of the waves actually generated.

The next useful comparison must derive a production timescale or a different environmental response from a common interaction, while retaining cumulative redshift, signal timing, energy conservation and matter-clock constraints. Choosing fast waves by hand would abandon the link just tested. Choosing a thinner or weaker barrier also changes its static suppression and must report that tradeoff. A real capture channel and a supported joint motion/lensing source remain required even if transmission improves.

This calculation does not prove an absolute shortage of photon-to-companion energy, identify companions as ordinary gravitons, establish a low-gravity environmental law, or construct a galaxy. All 20 tasks and 32 requirements remain in scope.
