# Accumulated redshift and timing through a shared field

## Question and result

Smaller conversion regions improved companion delivery in the preceding calculation, but also changed photon loss. This test puts one or three regions in a single evolving companion field and follows the driving photon through them without resetting anything. Infinitesimal signals then probe the same field at different launch times.

**Redshift accumulates, and carrier-frequency stretch matches local event-time stretch in this candidate. However, the stretch depends on the radiation loading and the launch time.** Multiplying an unchanged single-region factor is not the correct composition rule for the driving packet. A finite event also does not acquire one common affine stretch at all phases in the tested background.

This is progress toward a coupled propagation model, not a derivation of a universal redshift-distance law. The radiation history and environmental structure must be modeled before the calculation can predict astronomical signals.

## 1. One field, several regions

The Hamiltonian is the massless-field version of the earlier finite-source model:

```
H = integral K/2 [u^2 + v^2 phi_x^2] dx + P/n_bar(X),
n_bar(X) = 1 + integral W_sigma(x-X) g(x) phi(x) dx.
```

The envelope g is the sum of disjoint cosine-squared regions of half-width 0.5 centered at 0, 3 and 6; the single-region control retains only the first. The field initially has zero displacement, velocity and energy. The same field evolves across the entire domain throughout each run. Nothing is reinitialized as the photon reaches a new region.

The driving packet begins at x=-2 with energy 0.003 or 0.03. The field normalization is K=1, companion speed v=0.5 in c=1 units, and Gaussian smoothing width is 0.25. The domain is -20<x<40 and the end time is 25. Grids contain 1,920 and 3,840 cells, corresponding to spacings 0.03125 and 0.015625. Boundary-strip checks ensure that this observation interval does not use periodic recirculation.

Hamilton's equations are

```
phi_t = u,
u_t = v^2 phi_xx + [P/(K n_bar^2)] W_sigma g,
X_dot = 1/n_bar,
P_dot = (P/n_bar^2) partial_X n_bar.
```

The photon loses exactly the energy gained by this receiving field. Static conversion profiles can exchange momentum; the numerical momentum-rate ledger includes their force and the spatial-grid contribution. A dynamical material realization of the profiles remains missing. There is no restoring slab in this test: it isolates accumulation and signal timing, while the preceding slab calculations separately constrain delivery. Capture and a gravitational response have not been inserted.

## 2. Driving-packet accumulation

The finer-grid results are:

| Initial packet energy | One-region energy stretch | Three-region energy stretch | Frozen one-region factor cubed |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.003 | 1.000513718 | 1.001541155 | 1.001541947 |
| 0.030 | 1.005141851 | 1.015425475 | 1.015505006 |

Energy stretch means initial packet energy divided by final packet energy. It is a carrier-frequency prediction only under the specified fixed-number packet and energy-standard assumptions. Changing this classical packet energy changes radiation loading; it is not a controlled comparison of individual photon colors.

At the larger loading, cubing the frozen factor overpredicts the redshift increment by about 0.52%. The difference is larger than the observed grid change. The discrepancy is small in this weak example, but it demonstrates why a per-region factor cannot automatically remain fixed as the packet loses energy.

The weak-loss limit suggests a useful alternative diagnostic. If one region removes energy C E squared at leading order, repeated passages approximately satisfy

```
dE/dN = -C E^2,
1/E(N) = 1/E(0) + C N,
S(N) = E(0)/E(N) = 1 + C E(0) N.
```

Calibrating this approximation to the single-region result gives S(3) approximately 1+3[S(1)-1], or 1.015425554 in the larger-loading case, close to the coupled result. This comparison is not an independent fitted law or proof of validity for arbitrarily many regions. The code directly evolves the full system; the approximation is reported afterward to explain the behavior.

The general exponential integral of a state-dependent fractional-loss rate remains available. What is not established is a constant rate independent of radiation, signal state and history.

## 3. Weak signals in the actual background

Faint probes launch at times 0, 1, 3 and 6 from x=-2 and arrive at x=9. They sample the same spatially smoothed optical factor generated by the driving packet. Their energy tends to zero, so they do not alter that background. This approximation tests propagation; a finite-energy second packet would require its own backreaction in the Hamiltonian.

For the stipulated carrier Hamiltonian omega=k/n, two independently integrated descriptions are used. Time-parametrized rays satisfy

```
dx/dt = 1/n,
dk/dt = k n_x/n^2.
```

The arrival map and its launch-time derivative J are independently propagated in space:

```
dt/dx = n,
dJ/dx = n_t J,  J_at_source=1.
```

They imply d ln omega/dx = -n_t and thus a carrier-frequency stretch equal to J. The code checks both the arrival time and the stretch using the independent integrations. Endpoint optical factors are calculated rather than silently discarded. Source and detector matter-clock laws are still unspecified; the reported results use the common reference-time and frequency standard. The inherited clock-scaling controls in the output are illustrative alternatives, not validated atomic physics.

For initial driving energy 0.03, the finer-grid probe results are:

| Probe launch time | One-region frequency/local timing stretch | Three-region frequency/local timing stretch |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1.005141851 | 1.015425475 |
| 1 | 1.002263858 | 1.006704371 |
| 3 | 1.000001997 | 1.013662046 |
| 6 | approximately 1 | 1.006813419 |

The launch-zero probe follows the same trajectory as the driver and therefore supplies a useful consistency control. Later probes encounter a different field history. In the one-region example the late effect has essentially ceased; in the three-region example traveling disturbances from other regions can still cross a probe's path. These are effects of the finite radiation burst and shared field, not ordinary endpoint gravitational time dilation.

The three-region stretch is nonmonotonic with launch time. A whole transient cannot therefore be represented by one constant dilation factor in this example. Using successive launch times as the edges of an event interval gives duration factors approximately 1.014620 for [0,1], 1.005294 for [1,3], and 1.003729 for [3,6]. These are averages over different arrival-map intervals. A local carrier stretch at an endpoint need not equal the average stretch of a finite interval when the map is nonaffine. A full light-curve, spectral and detector calculation remains to be done.

## 4. Checks and limitations

Eight coupled backgrounds cover two region counts, two driving energies and two resolutions. Thirty-two carrier/event comparisons pass. The maximum relative energy drift is below 7e-14; the maximum carrier/timing disagreement is below 4e-15; independent arrival times differ by less than 2e-13 in the model's time units. Numerical momentum-rate and boundary-contamination checks also pass.

Grid refinement changes driver loss by less than 0.041% relatively and probe stretch by at most about 1.11e-5 absolutely. These are numerical diagnostics under the declared model, not observational error bars. The companion identity, physical region sizes, three-dimensional transport and matter response are not determined by these checks.

Most importantly, the background here is generated by one finite packet. Real source populations would supply a sustained, spatially distributed radiation history. This test does not show that a many-source background has the same timing variation, nor that it averages to a universal rate. Such averaging must be calculated with its energy source and fluctuations included.

## 5. Next physical requirement

A source population is now needed, rather than a freely assigned loss coefficient or a fresh field reset at each region. The next comparison should inject an explicitly accounted sequence of radiation packets into the common field and test whether weak-signal redshift and event stretching become stable across launch times. It must also measure fluctuations and dependence on mean source power; a favorable mean alone could hide unacceptable event distortion.

No fixed region count or width is adopted as an astronomical fact by this calculation. A physical environmental law must determine them. Clock compatibility, actual capture, stable deposited profiles and a common prediction of motion and lensing remain required. All 20 tasks and 32 requirements remain open to their existing completion standards.
