# What distinguishes the two vertical-gravity predictions?

**Velocities alone need not distinguish them.** A known one-dimensional isothermal equilibrium construction gives identical conditional vertical-velocity distributions in different potentials, with different intrinsic stellar-density profiles. This is a counterexample to reading gravity directly from a velocity spread, not a claim that all velocity information is useless.

## Known equilibrium equations and conditional construction

In a separable, time-independent one-dimensional vertical model, let K=dPhi/dz. The known stationary collisionless equation is v*d_z f-K*d_v f=0. The familiar isothermal solution f(z,v)=A exp[-(v^2/2+Phi(z))/sigma^2] satisfies this equation. It implies n(z)/n(0)=exp[-(Phi(z)-Phi(0))/sigma^2], while p(v|z) is the same Gaussian for any Phi at fixed sigma. These are established statistical-mechanical/stellar-dynamical formulas, not new companion physics.

We use piecewise-linear vertical-force surrogates through the stored R=8 kpc, bar-axis force knots from z=0 to 2 kpc. Their potentials are integrated exactly segment by segment. This is not a full three-dimensional Milky Way distribution function, a precise potential reconstruction between sparse knots, or a fit to observed tracer densities. Illustrative sigma values of 20, 40 and 60 km/s are not measured intrinsic dispersions from our catalogs. Both model tracer densities are normalized to the same midplane value.

## Explicit selection ambiguity

If observed counts are proportional to S(z)n(z), a different unknown positional selection S can compensate for the different intrinsic density. We construct valid illustrative functions with 0<S<=0.5 for both models; their normalized observed height distributions agree within 1.4e-15. Since the conditional velocity distributions also agree, the selected joint distributions agree in this example. These selection functions are constructed to demonstrate identifiability, not estimated for APOGEE or Gaia. Actual survey selection must be constrained rather than freely adjusted to rescue a gravity model.

The stationary collisionless identity cancels to below 8e-15 in direct evaluation. These numerical identities verify the mathematical example, not the validity of separability, equilibrium, isothermality or our actual Galactic potential.

## Required next observation work

The vertical test needs a shared tracer-population model and a constrained selection function, together with distance/proper-motion errors and the radial-vertical motion terms absent from this example. A forward model of intrinsic density, velocities and survey sampling can then ask which gravity field describes the observations. A raw bulge-versus-disk dispersion comparison cannot substitute for that calculation. Non-isothermal variation, cross moments and other observables may add constraints; the counterexample does not eliminate them.

No new observational sample is opened, and no independent evidence for either gravity model is produced. All six research objectives remain open.
