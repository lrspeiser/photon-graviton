# Fixed component deposits compared with exposed Cepheid motions

**The attached-component candidate improves on ordinary matter alone but underpredicts all nine scored bins.** RMS discrepancy is 28.41 km/s, versus 67.74 km/s for the original ordinary matter and 15.36 km/s for the earlier empirical extra field. No coefficient was fitted to these velocity outcomes in this calculation.

## Data and prediction

We use only the previously reduced and already exposed 167-star validation subset, retaining its original 12 bins, nine valid scored bins, spatial/velocity cuts and moment corrections. The saved selected-catalog hash is verified, and all bin counts agree. The final test role remains unopened. Because these validation outcomes have been examined previously, this is an exposed-data comparison, not fresh validation.

Coefficients are fixed from the inner component-source fit. Known linear Poisson gravity gives the sum of weighted component forces. To match the existing axisymmetric Jeans approximation, we use the azimuthal mean of the bar field and evaluate midplane radial balance at each selected star's radius. Bin prediction is sqrt(mean(-R a_R)); it is not the predicted speed of each individual star. This differs deliberately from the prior full three-dimensional force-vector check and cannot establish bar-streaming or vertical-motion agreement.

The inferred comparison speed comes from the prior simplified Jeans moment calculation, using fixed tracer scales, adopted distance calibration and solar frame, and incomplete treatment of mixed moments and selection. It is not a direct measurement of circular acceleration. This shared pipeline makes comparisons consistent, not free of systematic uncertainty.

## Physical interpretation

The density-fit coefficients do produce additional gravitational acceleration, so source-density mismatch alone was not a sufficient reason to dismiss the candidate. However, this fixed source does not reproduce the inferred motion trend under the shared reduction. The remaining RMS is not assigned a statistical significance because complete observational/model covariance is absent. Nor does a lower RMS than ordinary matter establish photon conversion as the cause.

The loading coefficients remain phenomenological. Incoming energy, capture efficiency, attachment dynamics, and storage stability are not derived. No new redshift, supernova timing, lensing or cluster result follows. Further work must test a physically specified transport/storage rule against motions and lensing together, preserving the final observational test. All six research objectives remain open.
