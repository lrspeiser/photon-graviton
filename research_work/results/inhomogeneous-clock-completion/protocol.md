# Operational clock completion with an evolving nonuniform lapse

This corresponds kinematically to the known lapse metric ds^2=-c0^2 dt^2/n^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2. No Einstein source equation or temporal-field energy completion is assumed. Freely falling observers need separate trajectories; fixed-position laboratories can require support.

Use the prescribed diagnostic profile n=1+beta*t*sin^2(pi*x), in units c0=1 and period one. This smooth periodic field is not a well-derived screening mechanism or an inferred void map. beta=0,0.02,0.2; emission coordinate time 1. Test paths (0,1), (0,2), (0,5), (0.25,1.25), (0.25,1). Retain endpoint clock factors on every path. No fitted astronomical parameter or outcome is used.

Integrate t'=n, log(omega)'=-partial_t n and arrival-map Jacobian J'=partial_t n*J along increasing x. Measured frequency is Omega=n*omega and measured event stretch is S=J*n_e/n_o. Compare to independently integrated proper-clock intervals of neighboring emissions, and the exact J=exp(beta*integral sin^2(pi*x) dx). Check local measured speed (dx/dt)/(d tau/dt)=1. Report holding-acceleration scale |partial_x ln n| along each path: local c preservation does not imply absence of gravitational forces.

The test closes clock/ray kinematics only. It does not certify tensor dynamics, a full energy/momentum ledger, wave diffraction, galaxy gravity or a radiation-generated spatial profile.
