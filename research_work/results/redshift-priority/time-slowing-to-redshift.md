# Explicit time-rate, light-stretch and measured-redshift mapping

Requested clarification, 9 September 2026. Provenance: optical-time reparametrization and the ray/clock ratio methods below are established mathematical constructions applied to our proposed field. The interaction interpretation is a candidate; no equation here is claimed unique. This note clarifies existing equations rather than adding a fitted physical law.

## 1. Define exactly what is slowing

Established optical-time construction, applied to the candidate:

q_opt(x,t) = 1/n(x,t),   d eta = q_opt dt.

The differential is a local optical phase-time relation; a general spatially varying q_opt need not define one global path-independent optical-time coordinate. t is the declared reference time. In the homogeneous case eta(t)=integral dt/n(t) is the optical phase clock used by the matched-wave model. It is not yet an atomic proper-time measurement.

With the proposed n=1+g phi, q_opt=1/(1+g phi). This last identification is an assumed optical coupling in our model, not a demonstrated universal slowdown of time. A decrease of q_opt means fewer optical phase cycles per unit reference time for fixed wave number.

Established fractional-rate definition: f=1-q_opt,o/q_opt,e. Positive f is the fractional slowing relative to the initial optical rate, not relative to an independently verified physical atomic clock.

## 2. Homogeneous field: amount of slowing to temporal stretch

Conditional consequence of the existing homogeneous optical law (established ratio algebra; no novelty claim):

S_ref = 1+z_ref = n_o/n_e = q_opt,e/q_opt,o = 1/(1-f).

nu_o/nu_e = 1-f,    T_o/T_e = 1/(1-f),

z_ref = f/(1-f),    f=z_ref/(1+z_ref).

Here T is a local carrier period; neighboring arrival intervals for a fixed path obey the same reference-time factor. A finite event follows that factor only if it is approximately constant throughout the event. Ten percent optical-rate slowing gives a 1/0.9 period factor, not a ten percent period increase. With unchanged Planck energy-frequency relation in the common energy standard, the lost photon energy fraction is f. In the agreed transfer rule, that amount enters the companion sector. Matter effects or other drivers still need their own energy terms.

| 1% | 1.010101 | 0.010101 | 1% |

These are illustrative consequences, not measured cosmic slowdowns or fitted results.

## 3. Spatially varying field: connect to the current root formula

Established ray identities applied to the proposed dispersion:

S_ref = exp(integral_ray alpha ds),

alpha = partial_t n/c0 = -partial_t q_opt/(c0 q_opt^2).

These equalities express the same candidate in rate-of-optical-time notation. They do not derive q_opt from void conditions. A spatially varying field generally does not reduce to the endpoint q_opt ratio; use the integral along the actual ray. For a stationary spatial profile, partial_t q_opt=0 and this mechanism produces no cumulative reference-frequency shift, though travel delay can change.

The fitted constant-alpha benchmark supplies a desired propagation rate empirically. The time-rate equation says what physical field evolution would be needed to supply it. The dynamical companion-field equations must generate that evolution from sources, environment and initial conditions without setting it from each target redshift.

## 4. Physical clocks determine the observed answer

Established clock-normalization accounting, conditional on identical transitions whose only reference-frequency change is described by q_atom:

1+z_measured = S_ref q_atom,o/q_atom,e.

q_atom is atomic transition cycles per unit reference time relative to the same baseline transition. A completed matter action must calculate it. Assuming it is fixed is a candidate protection condition, not an established fact about this model.

If atomic rates are protected at emission/detection, their ratio is one and measured redshift equals the propagation stretch minus one. If everything slows together so that q_atom=q_opt in the homogeneous case, the factors cancel and z_measured=0. Thus calling the optical change a universal slowdown of time would overclaim and can erase the signal we seek. This does not prove that all cumulative-time theories cancel; it exposes the necessity of specified matter/field couplings.

## 5. Longer periods versus longer spatial waves

In the homogeneous model omega=c0 k/n with conserved k, reference frequency falls and the period grows, but spatial wavelength stays fixed because light's reference-coordinate speed falls at the same time. That is a limitation of the current example. A measured lower frequency becomes a proportionately longer locally measured wavelength only when the local propagation speed and length standards used in that comparison are appropriately matched. Equal protected endpoint regions can provide a different boundary problem, but their source, detector and intervening field must be solved together; do not apply the homogeneous endpoint shortcut to that spatially varying case.

## Difference from the formulas already in use

The distance exponential is a fitted accumulation benchmark. The optical factor n describes the candidate propagation law. Introducing q_opt makes the implied local optical-time rate explicit and allows a fractional slowing to be translated into a temporal stretch. It is a rewriting of the same optical model, not a second independently validated mechanism. What is still missing is the dynamics plus matter coupling that make q_opt and q_atom measurable and predict the observed redshift without expansion. The user requested this distinction explicitly; future manuscript revisions should preserve it.
