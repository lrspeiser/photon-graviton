# Sky structure materially changes uncertainty sensitivity

The constant-rate baseline's saved coarse-sky out-of-fold predictions have RMS 450.716 km/s and overall bias -20.893 km/s, with residual defined as prediction minus observation. These are c times redshift differences, not independently measured peculiar velocities. All 164 exposed groups are retained; no model was refitted in this pass.

Regional mean residuals range from -531.597 to +208.356 km/s. Region 2 (RA 180-270 degrees, negative declination) has 16 groups, mean -531.597 km/s and RMS 776.123 km/s. This is a description of the predefined split, not a newly selected cut or evidence to remove that region. The eight regional summaries are retained in the result JSON. A near-zero full-sample mean does not mean the regional means are near zero.

## Conditional bootstrap sensitivity

Using 10,000 replicates and the fixed saved predictions:

| Resampling unit | RMS percentile range (km/s) | Bias percentile range (km/s) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Individual galaxy groups | 376.715 to 527.128 | -90.946 to +47.015 |
| Whole predefined sky regions | 361.512 to 560.861 | -168.266 to +95.419 |

These are exploratory 2.5th-to-97.5th percentile ranges for performance statistics conditional on the saved cross-validation predictions. They are **not** 95% predictive intervals for a new galaxy. The whole-region bias span is about 1.91 times the individual-group span. Eight regions provide limited resampling support; neither partition is proven to capture the physical correlation length, and overlapping cross-validation training sets add dependence not captured by merely resampling fixed predictions. We do not claim formal coverage or a complete parameter uncertainty from this bootstrap.

In plain language, galaxies sharing a direction can carry shared errors or shared physical effects. Counting each as independent can make the sample seem more informative than it is. This calculation establishes sensitivity to the counting unit, not whether motion, calibration, selection or our propagation law causes the pattern. It does not justify an adjustable correction for each galaxy or region.

## A calibration-size limit, not a new interval prescription

**Provenance: established split-conformal rank rule, not our formula.** For n exchangeable calibration scores, separate from model training, and requested coverage p, the usual augmented-score threshold uses rank k = ceil((n+1)p). When k exceeds n, the appended score is positive infinity. This standard framework and its exchangeability requirement are described by [Angelopoulos and Bates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511).

At 95% nominal coverage, seven calibration units require rank eight and eight units require rank nine: both thresholds are infinite. Nineteen calibration units permit rank nineteen, a finite threshold when their scores are finite. This is a mathematical minimum for that particular rule, not a sufficient sample size for reliable scientific calibration or a universal impossibility result for other uncertainty methods. If calibration units are whole sky regions, one must justify their exchangeability and define the region score and future target consistently.

The eight observed regions cannot supply 19 separate calibration regions merely by renaming their galaxies. Conversely, the 65 finer sky tiles are not proved exchangeable and independent just because there are more of them. The saved out-of-fold residuals were generated by different overlapping training sets; they are not a valid ordinary split-conformal calibration set for one separately fitted model. We have not applied that method to these data or claimed its guarantee.
