# Formula-to-observation workflow

User direction: prioritize formulas fitted to actual observations, followed by predictions for the next galaxy, system, or redshift sample. Do not run standalone exercises to establish how detailed the available data can become.

For each candidate:

1. State the equation, observable, units, assumptions, and shared free parameters. Label known mathematical relations, proposed physical rules, and empirical fits separately.
2. Declare the calibration objects and prediction objects before fitting. Record whether either set has already influenced model choices; an exposed sample is not a blind test.
3. Fit the calibration observations, using their uncertainties and available covariance. Separate stipulated distances and measured quantities from model-dependent reductions.
4. Freeze the shared parameters and predict the next objects or redshift range. Object-specific measured inputs are allowed; fitting the prediction target to rescue a mismatch is not.
5. Report observed value, predicted value, residual, and uncertainty, plus a consistent aggregate error measure and comparison with the retained formula.
6. Retain, reject, or explicitly revise the candidate according to transfer performance. A revision begins a new recorded candidate; it does not overwrite an unsuccessful prediction.

Numerical verification should support a specific calculation or resolve an identified reliability problem. Data acquisition should supply an identified missing observable for a declared comparison. Neither is a separate research objective.

Passing one comparison does not establish photon conversion as its cause. Shared predictions for redshift, timing, brightness, stellar motion, and lensing must be mutually consistent, and conservation and deposit support must remain explicit. Necessary physical consistency calculations should identify whether a candidate can proceed, not substitute for observational predictions.

No fixed cosmic age or size, expansion, dark matter, or Big Bang history is assumed as a physical premise of the hypothetical model. Where published reductions depend on such assumptions, disclose that dependency rather than labeling the inferred result a direct measurement.
