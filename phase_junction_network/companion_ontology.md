## Decision

For the first integrated Phase Junction/photon–companion construction, define the traveling **companion** as a quantum of the gauge-neutral local matter–geometry relative-phase field

\[
\varphi=\theta_m-\theta_g,
\]

not as a photon, not as the massless tensor graviton, and not as the connection-relative variable removed by the second-class lock.

A long-lived deposited companion reservoir, if it exists, is a **bound collective state of the same \(\varphi\) sector**, possibly stabilized by matter defects or topology. It is not a separately inserted halo field.

This is a provisional branch, not an established physical identity. It is selected because it yields the cleanest single-field route from photon/matter energy transfer to propagation, binding, storage, and gravitational stress-energy while keeping the electromagnetic and gravitational mediators distinct.

## Why this branch is preferred

### Photon as companion

Using the photon itself as the companion would not explain transfer out of the observed electromagnetic mode or the existence of a separate stored reservoir. It would also risk counting the same electromagnetic energy twice.

### Tensor graviton as companion

The tensor mode is the natural long-range gravitational mediator. A mechanism that captures and stores ordinary tensor gravitons strongly enough to build galaxy-scale reservoirs would also have to explain why gravitational radiation remains weakly interacting and propagates cleanly. Treating the tensor graviton as both mediator and accumulating matter-like reservoir would blur two roles that the current constraint construction keeps distinct.

### Connection-relative excitation as companion

The finite Weyl construction deliberately gaps the relative connection variables and removes them from the low-energy physical band. Reintroducing them as the principal traveling companion would reverse the purpose of the second-class reduction.

### Topological defect as the sole companion

A topological defect may be a useful stored object, but it is not yet a natural general carrier for smooth, repeated photon-energy transfer across arbitrary frequencies. It remains a candidate stabilizer or endpoint of the same \(\varphi\) sector.

### Neutral relative-phase mode

The neutral relative-phase field can consistently be:

- distinct from the two transverse photon polarizations;
- scalar and gauge neutral;
- gapped or nearly gapless depending on its Josephson potential;
- produced or absorbed through matter-assisted interactions;
- capable in principle of bound collective configurations;
- a source of the shared frame through its stress-energy;
- constrained by clock, fifth-force, spectral, and coherence observations.

Those properties do not prove the mechanism works. They make it a coherent first branch to test.

## Interaction rule

A bare term

\[
\varphi F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
\]

is a useful allowed effective interaction to analyze, but it does not by itself establish the desired one-photon redshift process in empty homogeneous vacuum. Energy–momentum and quantum-number accounting generally require at least one of:

- emitter or receiver recoil;
- a background or bound \(\varphi\) configuration;
- matter-assisted transitions;
- pair production;
- an inhomogeneous frame or junction background;
- a multiphoton process.

The active branch should therefore begin with a finite matter-assisted transition, not assume free single-photon conversion.

A schematic microscopic process is

\[
|e;\gamma_\omega;n_\varphi\rangle
\longleftrightarrow
|g;\gamma_{\omega'};n_\varphi+1\rangle,
\]

with

\[
\hbar\omega+E_e
=\hbar\omega'+E_g+E_\varphi+E_{\rm recoil}.
\]

Every rate calculation must retain the reverse process and all recoil or environmental energy.

## Propagation requirements

The companion dispersion must be derived from its finite or continuum Hamiltonian:

\[
\omega_\varphi^2(k)
=m_\varphi^2c_\varphi^4/\hbar^2
+c_\varphi^2k^2
+O(k^4\ell^2).
\]

The theory must calculate rather than stipulate:

- \(m_\varphi\) or the gap;
- propagation speed;
- dispersion and coherence;
- polarization content;
- spontaneous decay and reverse conversion;
- interaction with background matter and radiation;
- whether travel is effectively lossless over the required path.

The companion need not share the photon speed merely because photons and tensor modes share the frame cone. Equality or inequality is a prediction of the \(\varphi\) kinetic sector.

## Deposit and gravitational sourcing

A deposited reservoir is a bound solution

\[
\varphi_{\rm bound}(x,t)
\]

of the same companion equations, including its interactions with matter and the frame.

Its stress-energy must be derived by variation with respect to the shared frame:

\[
T_{\mu\nu}^{(\varphi)}
=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}
\frac{\delta S_\varphi}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.
\]

The same resulting geometry must govern:

- stellar and gas motion;
- light bending and delay;
- clock rates;
- gravitational-wave propagation;
- the companion's own bound-state support.

No independent motion multiplier or lensing multiplier is permitted.

## Clock and matter response

Material clocks are transitions of the same matter defects used as emitters and receivers. A local companion background can affect a clock only through derived dependence of defect masses or couplings, for example

\[
m_s(\varphi),
\qquad
q_s(\varphi),
\qquad
\Delta E_{ab}(\varphi).
\]

This makes clock effects composition- and transition-dependent unless symmetry forces universality. Such dependence must be calculated and tested; it cannot be hidden inside a coordinate-time redefinition.

## First falsification sequence

### Gate 1: finite source transition

Build the smallest finite matter/link/\(\varphi\) Hilbert space that permits the forward and reverse transition. Verify exact charge, energy, momentum, and occupation accounting.

### Gate 2: spectral law

Derive the frequency and environmental dependence of the transfer rate. Reject the branch if the required cumulative redshift demands already-excluded color dependence, broadening, or noise.

### Gate 3: free propagation

Propagate a finite companion packet and measure speed, dispersion, decay, spreading, and reverse conversion.

### Gate 4: bound state

Demonstrate a finite-energy bound configuration with a calculated formation rate, capacity, lifetime, release channel, momentum support, and binding-energy ledger.

### Gate 5: shared gravity

Use the bound state's derived stress-energy to predict both massive-body motion and light bending. No separate fit functions.

### Gate 6: end-to-end history

Generate the companion from ordinary source fuel, propagate it, capture it, source the frame, and receive the modified photon or matter signal with one frozen parameter set.

## Rejection conditions

Reject or revise this companion identification if:

- the finite theory cannot produce \(\varphi\) from ordinary matter and photons without inserted seeds;
- the rate necessarily has unacceptable color dependence, decoherence, broadening, or reverse-event noise;
- a long-lived traveling mode cannot coexist with efficient capture;
- bound configurations require an independently supplied halo or arbitrary external trap;
- the stress-energy produces motion but not the corresponding lensing response;
- the required clock shifts violate the same matter model's local behavior;
- source energy or angular momentum falls short without double counting;
- the companion must be alternately reidentified as a tensor graviton, photon, or connection mode to explain different observations.

## Alternatives retained

If this branch fails, preserve the result and test alternatives in this order:

1. a bound topological excitation of the same relative-phase sector;
2. a composite of relative-phase quanta and matter defects;
3. a different declared microscopic field with explicit evidence that the Phase Junction state space is incomplete.

Do not silently revert to identifying the companion with the massless tensor mode or an independently inserted dark sector.
