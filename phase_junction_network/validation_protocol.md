## 1. Research rule

No single successful spectrum, finite event, fit, or regulator verifies the theory. The required sequence is

\[
\text{ontology and finite variables}
\rightarrow
\text{physical phases and constraints}
\rightarrow
\text{shared matter and coefficients}
\rightarrow
\text{nonlinear/interacting continuum closure}
\rightarrow
\text{frozen predictions}
\rightarrow
\text{real-data tests}.
\]

Real measurements are initially exclusion walls, not flexible targets used to choose the action. A claim advances only when upstream dependencies, free parameters, and claim boundaries are explicit.

## 3. Ontology and integration gate

One state/field dictionary must distinguish:

- compact electromagnetic link phase and electric imbalance;
- frame variables and constrained connection variables;
- the neutral relative-phase companion `chi=varphi`;
- charged and neutral matter defects;
- bound many-body companion deposits;
- global or boundary variables such as volume constraints.

Every physical energy contribution must appear exactly once. Transformations between sectors require explicit reciprocal terms.

Issue #7 passes only at the **finite architecture** level. Its committed model has a source, high- and low-frequency photon bins, companion excitation, matter recoil, a bound mode, capture recoil, a receiver, Hermitian reverse channels, and one frame for motion and lensing. It does not establish physical continuum rates, a common cone, many-body capacity, cosmological lifetime, or an observational fit.

## 7. Shared-parameter gate

One finite microscopic move set must derive or constrain

\[
U_A,\ K_A,\ U_g,\ K_g,\ r_0,\ \eta,\ \ell,
\]

plus matter hopping, defect gaps, companion conversion/binding/recoil couplings, and volume parameters.

Define

\[
Z_A=\sqrt{U_A/K_A},
\qquad
Z_g=\sqrt{U_g/K_g}.
\]

A common limiting speed constrains products, not the impedance ratio. Equality of `Z_A` and `Z_g` is optional until derived.

Parameter closure requires:

- fewer independent microscopic ratios than low-energy outputs;
- all perturbative denominators and combinatorial factors recorded;
- omitted-order estimates;
- no use of measured `G`, `alpha`, masses, or redshift coefficients to select microscopic amplitudes before relations are derived;
- at least one held-out cross-sector relation.

The issue-#2 self-dual clock point, issue-#4 localization inputs, and issue-#7 finite couplings are not yet such a derivation.

## 10. Rejection criteria before data fitting

Reject or revise a candidate if any of the following occurs:

- a physical negative-energy or negative-residue mode;
- `omega^2<0` near the vacuum;
- extra unsuppressed photon or tensor polarizations;
- a finite electromagnetic model that remains confined or gapped;
- tensor dispersion `omega~k^2` or `omega~k^3` in the intended relativistic phase;
- uncontrolled finite-state boundary occupation;
- low-energy leakage into the connection-relative band;
- loss of charge, energy, momentum, or stress-energy conservation;
- a hidden or silently discarded matter mirror;
- species-dependent gravity without a complete additional-force theory;
- separately tuned photon, companion, matter, and tensor cones;
- a volume term deleted by hand;
- an astrophysical conversion rate, lifetime, capture law, halo, or mass hierarchy inserted without dynamics;
- independent response functions for motion and lensing;
- no stable interacting continuum phase;
- more adjustable parameters than independent outputs.

## 11. Synthetic test ladder

### A. Vacuum and phase diagram

Map gaps, residues, order parameters, flux sectors, and phase boundaries across couplings, local dimensions, and regulator choices.

### B. Static sources

Use finite conserved electric charge and energy sources. Measure sign, range, anisotropy, finite-size corrections, and species dependence.

### C. Wave packets and correlators

Measure dispersion, speed, polarization, leakage, birefringence, tensor/vector/scalar content, and reflection positivity.

### D. Matter response

Propagate multiple defect species through identical electromagnetic and frame backgrounds. Test charge response, universal free fall, clocks, binding energy, mirror behavior, and radiative corrections.

### E. Radiation and reactions

Drive electric dipole, gravitational quadrupole, and photon–companion conversion processes. Verify reciprocal backreaction, emitted power, forbidden multipoles, and all ledgers.

### F. Nonlinear evolution

Evolve physical and constraint-violating data separately. Physical data must preserve constraints without repeated manual projection.

### G. End-to-end many-body companion experiment

Start from microscopic matter and photons in the actual finite EM phase, generate the companion, propagate it, capture/store many excitations, release or equilibrate them, source the nonlinear frame, and detect both matter and light response with one parameter set.

## 12. Freeze before empirical testing

Commit a protocol containing:

- exact microscopic Hamiltonian or action;
- vacuum and phase definition;
- complete parameter ledger;
- derived versus calibrated quantities;
- calibration observables;
- held-out predictions;
- source/data provenance;
- numerical and statistical tolerances;
- prohibited post-unblinding modifications;
- code commit and input/output hashes.

The first empirical package should test local and weak-field consequences before flexible galaxy or cosmological models:

1. photon, companion, matter, and gravitational propagation cones;
2. free-fall universality;
3. Solar-System motion, light bending, delay, and redshift;
4. material clock response;
5. gravitational-wave polarization and dispersion;
6. precision electromagnetic cutoff operators and coupling variation;
7. binary radiation and strong-field consistency.

## 13. Distinctive-prediction requirement

The theory is not empirically distinct if it reproduces QED and general relativity by independently selecting their coefficients. It must derive at least one pre-registered relation such as

\[
\mathcal R(\alpha,G,m_s,c,\text{cutoff coefficients},\text{companion response})=0
\]

that is not used for calibration.
