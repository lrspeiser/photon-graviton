# Constrained Local Reduction Without a TT Projector

**Status:** linear local-to-physical bridge passed  
**Date:** 2026-09-20

## 1. Purpose

The earlier work established two separate facts:

1. the local frame–connection action eliminates to the Fierz–Pauli frame stiffness;
2. a reduced two-polarization tensor sector has a positive finite transfer matrix.

The missing bridge was to derive the four-dimensional physical phase space directly from the **local frame and connection variables**, rather than inserting a transverse-traceless projector by hand.

This note performs that reduction at the linear level.

## 2. Extended local phase space

For each nonzero spatial momentum, use

\[
x=(h_I,p_I,C_a,P_a),
\]

with

- six symmetric frame components \(h_I\);
- six conjugate frame momenta \(p_I\);
- eighteen torsion-free connection components \(C_a\);
- eighteen conjugate connection momenta \(P_a\).

The extended phase space therefore has dimension

\[
6+6+18+18=48.
\]

Let

\[
C=A(k)h
\]

be the linearized Levi-Civita relation, where the \(18\times6\) matrix \(A(k)\) is the Fourier representation of

\[
\Gamma^a{}_{bc}[h]
=\frac12\left(
\partial_bh^a{}_c+
\partial_ch^a{}_b-
\partial^ah_{bc}
\right).
\]

Let \(J_C\) be the nondegenerate quadratic form of the independent connection. The first-order quadratic Hamiltonian block is

\[
K_{hC}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -4A^TJ_C\\
-4J_CA & 4J_C
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The frame momentum block is

\[
T=I-\frac12tt^T,
\]

where \(t_I\) extracts the spatial trace.

## 3. The connection constraints are second-class

Because the connection has no independent velocity in this linear first-order branch, its momenta give eighteen primary constraints,

\[
\phi_a=P_a\approx0.
\]

Preserving them in time gives

\[
\dot P_a=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial C_a}
=-4(J_C)_{ab}(C_b-A_{bI}h_I)\approx0.
\]

Since \(J_C\) has rank 18, this produces eighteen secondary constraints,

\[
\psi_a=C_a-A_{aI}h_I\approx0.
\]

Their Poisson-bracket matrix is equivalent to

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -I_{18}\\
I_{18} & 0
\end{pmatrix}
\]

and therefore has rank 36.

Thus

\[
\boxed{P_C=0,\quad C=\Gamma[h]}
\]

form a complete second-class set. The independent connection does not survive as a propagating particle.

## 4. Four extended first-class constraints

Let \(R(k)\) be the \(6\times3\) map generating spatial frame relabelings,

\[
\delta h=R\xi,
\]

and let

\[
s_Ih_I=0
\]

be the scalar curvature constraint.

The vector generators must transform both the frame and the independent connection:

\[
\widetilde G_i
=R_{Ii}p_I+(AR)_{ai}P_a.
\]

The scalar constraint is

\[
S=s_Ih_I.
\]

The added \((AR)P\) term is essential. It ensures

\[
\delta(C-Ah)=AR\xi-A(R\xi)=0.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\{\widetilde G_i,\phi_a\}
=\{\widetilde G_i,\psi_a\}
=\{S,\phi_a\}
=\{S,\psi_a\}=0.
\]

The first-class constraints also commute among themselves at the linear level because

\[
s^TR=0.
\]

The numerical implementation verifies these identities over every tested nonzero lattice momentum.

## 6. Second-class elimination

Embedding the second-class surface,

\[
C=Ah,\qquad P=0,
\]

back into the extended symplectic form leaves the ordinary canonical frame form,

\[
\Omega_{\rm frame}=dh_I\wedge dp_I.
\]

The pulled-back Hamiltonian is

\[
H_{\rm red}
=\frac12p^TTp+rac12h^TM_{\rm FP}(k)h,
\]

where

\[
M_{\rm FP}(k)=-4A^TJ_CA.
\]

This reproduces the prior exact Schur-complement identity, but now as the explicit elimination of a full second-class constraint pair.

## 7. First-class quotient without a TT projector

Let the reduced frame phase-space vector be

\[
y=(h,p)\in\mathbb R^{12},
\]

and let \(F\) be the \(4\times12\) matrix of the three vector constraints and one scalar constraint.

The algorithm is:

1. Find a basis \(N\) for the constraint surface,
   \[
   FN=0.
   \]
   For nonzero momentum, \(N\) has eight columns.
2. Pull back the symplectic form,
   \[
   \Omega_N=N^T\Omega N.
   \]
3. The nullspace of \(\Omega_N\) is exactly the four-dimensional gauge orbit generated by
   \[
   \Omega^{-1}F^T.
   \]
4. Retain the four non-null singular directions of \(\Omega_N\). They form the quotient phase space.
5. Pull back the Hamiltonian to that quotient and solve
   \[
   \Omega_{\rm phys}\dot z=H_{\rm phys}z.
   \]

At no point does the code construct or insert a transverse-traceless projector.

## 10. Zero mode

The calculation intentionally excludes \(k=0\) from the propagating-mode test. At zero momentum the derivative constraints lose rank and the frame potential vanishes. Those modes are the previously identified periodic global frame sector, not finite-momentum gravitons.

A later nonlinear and boundary-condition analysis must determine whether they are gauge, moduli, conserved global geometry, or lifted by additional physics.
