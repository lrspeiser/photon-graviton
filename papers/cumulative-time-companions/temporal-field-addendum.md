# Temporal-field mechanism addendum — 12 September 2026

This working addendum updates the mechanism assessment alongside the historical v0.5 manuscript/PDF. It does not certify a complete new observational model. The early mechanism tests are synthetic operational experiments with reference-energy ledgers. Subsequent capture sections include explicitly labeled calibration against exposed stellar-motion proxies; none constitutes independent validation or a complete physical conservation proof.

## Clock completion and energy transport

The [inhomogeneous clock calculation](../../research_work/results/inhomogeneous-clock-completion/report.md) shows that specified evolving nonuniform fields can preserve observable spectral/event stretching with the clock rule d tau=dt/n and fixed spatial rods. This is a conditional kinematic result using known mathematics, not a radiation-derived source history. Uniform temporal changes cancel in these clocks. Spatial gradients also imply force effects that cannot be omitted from a later gravity model.

The [field-funded transport calculation](../../research_work/results/field-funded-transport/report.md) supplies a moving field-energy sector and avoids a previously identified empty-companion energy deficit in a tested domain. Its parameter choice keeps the temporal sector's energy flux slower than light explicitly. Conservation and clock tests pass, but net radiation energy increases in its seeded examples; their deposits cannot be described as net photon-funded.

## Radiation-powered initiation

The [initiation experiment](../../research_work/results/radiation-powered-initiation/report.md) adds the explicit postulate I=epsilon R for whole-photon conversion into the companion sector. It starts from zero companion/temporal energy and produces positive net radiation-funded deposits without negative reservoirs in the tested trajectories. Whole-photon removal is attenuation, not a frequency shift of survivors. A distinct evolving temporal field must supply their spectral and event stretch.

With two prescribed capture regions, the lower-rate example yields early measured z=0.001073 and photon survival 0.904657; its late measured z is -0.000858. The higher-rate example has early z=0.003730, survival 0.603870 and late z=-0.003427. These nondimensional synthetic cases are not calibrated to galaxies. Less than one percent of their integrated net radiation transfer is temporal work; most is whole-photon conversion.

The late coordinate event Jacobian approaches one as the field settles. Under this operational clock rule, standard calculus gives log(1+z)=-integral partial_x(log n) dx along the evolving ray; in a stationary field this reduces to an endpoint ratio. Continuous conversion and capture alone therefore do not establish persistent accumulated redshift.

The [bar-torque experiment](../../research_work/results/bar-capture-torques/report.md) reuses the conditional ordinary bar density without halo or deposited additions. Eight selected radial probes enter0.1kpc in its spherical monopole but turn outside that radius on their first approach in the stationary and rotating bar. Angular momentum is generated by standard nonspherical gravity; the rotating case satisfies Delta E=Omega Delta Lz to the numerical tolerance. Field-order and integrator checks pass. This shows that the spherical zero-torque assumption need not persist in an ordinary bar; it does not establish long-term core avoidance, a capture-weighted population, or a complete Milky Way model. Historical mass-model dependence is retained explicitly.

The [coupled formation experiment](../../research_work/results/coupled-radial-formation/report.md) starts from zero deposits and combines depleted capture, momentum-derived radial injection, motion and evolving deposited gravity. The lower rate passes numerical mass/fraction and mechanical checks over the tested interval; the higher rate reaches relativistic speeds and rapidly changing opacity, invalidating the nonrelativistic/quasistatic approximation regardless of individual numerical passes. Lower-rate central depth remains sensitive to numerical regularization despite stable outer fractions. Incoming-energy ledgers and discrete gravitational insertion work are separated; no global source funding or stable final galaxy is claimed. A physical central/phase-space treatment, causal validity and the photon/time source remain open.

The [radial transport response](../../research_work/results/radial-capture-response/report.md) follows a negligible continuously injected bound population in a frozen spherical potential. Inward capture motions change the central added-mass fraction by roughly96-fold at T30 relative to permanent deposition at arrival positions; the projected central fraction changes10.34-fold, providing a distinct conditional lensing input. Orbital energy is conserved while kinetic energy increases through infall. All declared numerical gates pass. This response is per infinitesimal source amplitude, not a finite-mass formation solution; changing gravity, attenuation and background response have not been evolved. It demonstrates why the retained profile must be derived dynamically before interpreting a galaxy calibration.

The [capture-injection audit](../../research_work/results/capture-injection/report.md) supplies one candidate local momentum rule: photon-like incoming E/c combines into a cold massive cohort without an external impulse or outgoing energy. Standard four-momentum composition gives inward beta=|F|/J and a rest-energy fraction sqrt(1-beta^2); independent angular and continuity calculations agree within the stated gates. Low-opacity cohorts can start bound, but higher-opacity examples mostly do not at the selected weak-field scales. Local mean tangential momentum vanishes, so this rule does not assemble the earlier circular-orbit support. The result is an instantaneous source diagnostic on old profiles, not a self-consistent formation solution; global gravitational energy, source transport and the photon/time mechanism remain unresolved.

The [retention audit](../../research_work/results/deposit-retention/report.md) conditionally treats the stored state as massive bound particles after supply is switched off. A shielded profile violates the necessary monotonic-density condition for isotropic f(binding energy)>=0; formal Jeans support also requires central mean squared speeds about6512 times the bound limit. A randomly oriented circular-orbit ensemble supplies an explicit frozen spherical alternative, with positive circular binding and radial test-orbit frequencies. Orbital kinetic, self-potential and ordinary-field interaction energies are computed. These known mechanics relations do not prove formation, collective stability or energy/momentum closure during capture; a stored-field alternative remains distinct. Final numerical refinement passes after recorded initial failures, and no new observational data were accessed.

The [depleted-supply calculation](../../research_work/results/depleted-capture-feedback/report.md) replaces uniform unattenuated capture with isotropic chord absorption. It separates C=B*kappa0 into incident mass-equivalent fluence and opacity; fixing C does not fix the available incident energy. A derived absorption ledger bounds deposits by B*pi*R^2, with transmitted energy tracked, but several branches remain boundary-dependent. The ledger omits transport-time, momentum and support/binding sectors, so it is not a full conservation proof. Initial numerical failures and subsequent refinement outcomes are retained:16/18 cases pass the primary gate, with two negligible central-force contributions unresolved in relative accuracy. A conditional photon-like momentum extension gives an inward absorbed force; it cannot supply outward mechanical support. Mechanical retention and connection to a common photon/time source remain unresolved.

The subsequent [steep-tail feedback experiment](../../research_work/results/integrable-capture-feedback/report.md) tests p4/p6 in a spherical Plummer diagnostic with no observed-data fit. Weak-source cases have converged mass, depth and selected forces over the tested domain range. Strong-source cases nearly saturate capture throughout the domain: mass and central depth scale approximately as R^3 and R^2, respectively, while inner forces appear converged. This verifies that a formally integrable low-depth law is a conditional repair, not sufficient evidence of a finite-mass coupled solution at arbitrary exposure. Numerical refinement and analytic gravity checks pass; source depletion, binding/support and the joint observation model remain open.

The [capture-boundary audit](../../research_work/results/coupled-capture-boundary/report.md) compares 30, 100 and 200 kpc cutoffs, retaining both transferred and recalibrated amplitudes. Recalibration hides a roughly 426-fold p3 deposited-mass range behind RMS values of 18.72–23.26 km/s. All refinement gates pass, but even the best case remains systematically too weak inside and too strong outside. Under isolated finite-mass, uniform infinite illumination and permanent in-place retention assumptions, the proposed low-depth p2/p3 laws have nonintegrable mass tails. This conditional power-counting result requires explicit outer physics, not an arbitrary fitted cutoff; redshift and full energy/retention closure remain unresolved.

The subsequent [coupled one-amplitude calibration](../../research_work/results/coupled-capture-calibration/report.md) fits C while evolving the deposited gravity, rather than reusing the no-feedback amount. It reduces the p3 training RMS to22.62km/s but retains an inner deficit of41km/s and outer excess of29km/s. The selected100kpc profile requires about3e13 solar masses in deposits. This is a conditional training fit with verified numerical refinement, not a recovered joint model; boundary and retention physics, source energy and independent tests remain unresolved.

The [chronological feedback audit](../../research_work/results/milky-way-capture-feedback/report.md) then lets accumulated deposits deepen the capture-controlling well. At the earlier fitted exposure normalization, p3 training RMS grows from8.76 to370.33km/s as the density approaches the saturated rate across most of the capture volume. This supersedes any interpretation of that no-feedback calibration as a consistent coupled model. A refined time-integration check resolves an initial failed numerical gate. A new calibration must evolve the feedback from zero deposits; an equilibrium density substitution or silent baryons-only capture restriction would not be equivalent.

The [Milky Way depth-capture calibration](../../research_work/results/milky-way-depth-capture/report.md) applies an optically thin depth law to the existing axisymmetric ordinary-matter field. One amplitude per variant is fitted to12 summaries of542 already processed training Cepheids. The p3 variant lowers unweighted training RMS75.24 to8.76km/s, without resolving inner residuals or testing new stars. Its deposited potential is several times the ordinary-matter depth at the Sun, invalidating treatment of capture feedback as negligible. The reused Bovy vertical-force comparison is excluded as an accepted validation claim because its inferred forces use a dark-halo potential family. Coupled capture, source energy, support and admissible vertical/lensing tests remain open.

The [capture-depth comparison](../../research_work/results/capture-depth-law/report.md) tests three common opacity exponents at matched central optical depths, retaining all radial predictions. A thin p3 case changes deposit-only circular speed by7.6% over radii3..9, but declines17.2% over9..27 when extended. Its small interception fraction and changing projected-to-enclosed mass ratio remain independent supply and lensing requirements. These are conditional shape calculations, not an observed rotation-curve fit or evidence for a microscopic exponent.

The [isotropic absorption calculation](../../research_work/results/isotropic-capture-profile/report.md) derives a deposited profile from all-direction incident intensity and a specified saturating binding-depth capture coefficient, rather than imposing a Plummer deposit profile. Strong absorption produces an outer layer and central depletion; spherical shell forces and projected lensing then provide different diagnostics. The common-depth-normalized examples are conditional shape comparisons, not a fit to stars or a proof of photon supply. Energy interception, projected-mass quadrature and clock checks pass, while formation, support and disk/bar geometry remain unresolved.

The [deposit-clock-gravity diagnostic](../../research_work/results/deposit-clock-gravity/report.md) connects a prescribed positive Plummer deposit profile to weak-field stellar acceleration, metric clocks and lensing. Growing wells give positive net spectral/event stretch, including endpoint clocks, in twelve tested histories. The effect is a known evolving-potential mechanism; the proposed deposited-energy source remains hypothetical. Mass doubling over the illustrative crossing produces z about5e-7 or5e-6 at initial potential depths1e-6 or1e-5 in the equal-potential metric variant. A time-only metric gives half its static light bending at fixed dynamical potential. Formation, support, capture-funded growth and gravitational potential-energy bookkeeping are not thereby solved.

The subsequent [slow-response audit](../../research_work/results/slow-response-history/report.md) increases the same response time by factors of ten and one hundred with unchanged initiation and capture. Both cases approach a stationary traveling-sector profile and yield late blueshift. An independently solved stationary flux system reproduces the fine-grid late shifts within 3.69e-7 across the control and extensions. Deposits continue growing, but their gravitational feedback is absent from this calculation. Slower response alone therefore does not establish sustained stretching in this finite-channel family; the next coupled derivation must include deposit-to-gravity/clock response or a specified evolving common background.

These tests strengthen the distinction among energy supply, surviving-photon frequency change and event timing. They do not complete the spatial gravity/lensing law, physical stress-energy/momentum accounting, an isotropic radiation history, or a joint real-data prediction. The active nine-goal roadmap remains incomplete. No formula in this addendum is certified as unique; proposed source terms are labeled postulates, and the ray/conservation relations are conditional applications of known mathematics. A sustained source history and its epoch dependence must be derived before a new model calibration or confirmatory test.
