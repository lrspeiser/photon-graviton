# Evaluate the invented candidate on its own equations and observations

Owner clarification, 20 September 2026: invent the gravity/companion interaction; use real data to test it, not agreement with older gravity formulas as a pass/fail requirement. This applies to all remaining work toward the twelve-item goal.

1. A new candidate is accepted or rejected using its explicitly declared laws, internal conservation and mathematical consistency, plus direct observable predictions. Agreement with Newtonian gravity, general relativity, MOND or a dark-matter fit is not itself a gate. Those models may appear as labeled context/benchmark predictions on the same data; they cannot supply the answer the candidate is required to reproduce.
2. Established calculus, Hamiltonian mechanics, statistics and numerical methods remain available as mathematical tools, with attribution. A choice of shared effective metric, dispersion, standard relativistic kinematics or particular constitutive response is a candidate assumption, not an unavoidable consequence of naming a field a graviton. If changed, derive the complete replacement and its energy/momentum, clock and observable consequences.
3. Derive acceleration, propagation and lensing from the new interaction. Do not insert a known rotation law, a reconstructed halo profile or an independent lens multiplier as the target field. Fit shared candidate constants to declared training observations; test untouched observations without per-object law changes.
4. Clearly separate raw measured quantities from gravity-dependent mass/lensing reconstructions and cosmology-dependent distances. Prefer measured positions, redshifts with documented interpretation, velocities, spectra, delays and image shapes; reconstruct through the candidate when a published derived quantity assumes another theory.
5. RB-1's energy-budget rejection is conditional on SR-1's weak-wave source equations. It does not reject all conversion, attached-companion or nonlinear-response theories. Likewise SR-1's equal temporal/spatial response is a declared candidate choice, not success merely because it recovers a standard coefficient. Its observational success remains untested.
6. Attachment, coherent conversion and release are distinct mechanisms. A released companion may take a different path if the declared dynamics account for energy, momentum, binding and recoil. Release does not automatically mean random scattering: derive coherent/deterministic versus stochastic behavior and predict any image width. Earlier stochastic hitchhiking failures reject those tested laws, not every attachment law.
7. A conversion rate may depend on local field state, matter motion or alignment. It cannot look up a desired orbit or use the observation being predicted as its input. All feedback terms and their reciprocal reactions belong in the same model.
8. Literature is used to avoid false novelty claims and to identify conditional assumptions. A theorem about an older framework is not an automatic veto of a different fictional framework; state which assumptions it shares or replaces. Preserve unsuccessful variants and their limited scope.

The goal remains one source-funded model with stable stellar motion, cluster lensing, light quality/local tests, independently traceable data, shared held-out predictions and reproducible evidence. This clarification changes the judging criteria, not that scope.
