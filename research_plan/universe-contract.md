# Fictional-universe contract

This contract implements the user's explicit direction of 9 September 2026 and supersedes earlier unresolved distance questions and broader permission to explore alternative cosmological premises.

## Fixed facts and constraints

- Published galaxy distances are accepted as facts of this fictional universe. Preserve the adopted catalog values in each declared dataset; do not re-infer or tune them to improve a candidate fit. This is an explicit exception even when a catalog originally obtained its distance through a Hubble-flow calculation. Accepting the number does not import the calculation's expansion theory.
- The universe is operationally nonexpanding. Source separations must not grow merely because a variable was renamed or material rods were rescaled.
- Do not assume a Big Bang, a primordial dark-matter population, an independently supplied dark-matter halo, or an expanding background as an active explanatory premise.
- Conserve energy, including source fuel, traveling energy, deposits, fields, recoil, receiving sectors and boundary flow as applicable.
- Investigate the user's photon-to-companion/graviton-to-deposit mechanism. Any gravitating deposited reservoir must be derived from the permitted energy supply and interactions; inserting a conventional dark-matter halo under a different label does not satisfy this requirement.

Published distance uncertainties remain recorded metadata, not permission to fit distances away from their adopted values. Where catalogs quote different distances for the same object, retain the provenance and explicitly state which published value a particular analysis uses. The existing baseline is preserved; this decision does not silently change its source-coordinate choices or resolve cross-catalog discrepancies.

## Observations versus excluded interpretation

| Keep as a target or permitted input | Do not import as an active premise |
|---|---|
| Adopted published galaxy distances | The Hubble-flow/expansion derivation behind a distance |
| Measured spectral shifts, fluxes, sky positions and time histories, with instrument/processing metadata | Expansion as the assumed cause of redshift or time dilation |
| Rotation measurements and light-deflection data | A fitted dark-matter density/profile as an independently measured source |
| Microwave intensity and angular-correlation measurements | A Big-Bang/recombination origin, expansion-derived acoustic ruler or fitted cosmological parameters as facts |
| Galaxy clustering and observed correlation features | A standard early-universe acoustic interpretation or sound-horizon length without a new derivation |
| Element abundance measurements and stellar observations | Big-Bang nucleosynthesis yields or an expansion-derived cosmic age as required initial conditions |
| Processed data whose relevant assumptions are compatible or separately justified | Cosmology-dependent corrections and derived quantities whose excluded assumptions have not been removed/recomputed |

Do not discard an observation simply because conventional cosmology also explains it. Conversely, calling a published inference “data” does not make its theoretical assumptions admissible. Audit the measurement reduction where it matters. Distances are the explicit user-authorized exception; it does not automatically extend to every published mass, age, distance modulus or cosmological parameter.

## Consequences for existing work

The archived energy shortfalls are comparisons under their stated ordinary-gravity and source-history assumptions. An inferred “extra mass” is not a fixed dark-matter quantity the new theory may insert; the eventual target is the observed motion and lensing. The numerical energy ledger remains useful, and the actual energy supplied by photons still has to balance.

Earlier expanding conformal interpretations, primordial-background histories and independent dark-sector candidates are historical comparisons only. A candidate that requires those premises is outside the active search. A scalar or tensor field action is not excluded merely by its name: it must be evaluated on a permitted background with explicitly derived sources. The independent massive-particle reservoir in candidate C is inactive as a proposed substitute for the requested mechanism; it cannot be reinstated merely by renaming dark matter.

No-loss companion travel and permanent deposits remain optional hypotheses from the user's earlier message, not established laws. The age/source history, microscopic interaction, stored-state support, matter-clock behavior and gravitational response still need derivation. Negative findings within allowed branches should be explained in plain language with options for revision; the excluded premises above are no longer open alternatives for the active theory.
