# Clarification: stretching during travel through low-gravity voids

## User's intended mechanism

The user means that time operates differently in low-gravity voids, and that exposure during a journey billions of light-years long progressively stretches the traveling light. This must not be reduced to a claim solely about the source clock or to a claim that local galaxies have no redshift. Whether the void clock runs faster or slower, and how its behavior couples to light, remain quantities to derive rather than assume.

## The distinction that must be tested

In an ordinary static gravitational field, different clock rates do not by themselves produce an energy loss proportional to time spent at a fixed potential. For stationary emitters and observers, writing the metric clock factor as N(x), local proper time is d tau=N(x) dt and the frequency ratio is

    nu_observed / nu_emitted = N_emitter / N_observer.

For successive stationary regions the intermediate factors cancel. With the same clock factor at emission and reception, a static intervening void produces no net frequency shift by this mechanism, however long the path. It can change travel time without stretching the separation between identical successive signals. This statement assumes static geometry, stationary endpoints and ordinary nondissipative light propagation; moving or evolving structures require a different calculation. A low local gravitational acceleration is also not, by itself, a specification of the gravitational potential or clock factor.

The general stationary redshift-potential framework is described in [Perlick, Gravitational Lensing from a Spacetime Perspective](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5255571/). This is a comparison with ordinary gravity, not an imposed expanding-universe premise.

## How to express the intended cumulative alternative

One optional phenomenological law would be

    d ln(nu) / d ell = -alpha_void(local fields, frequency, epoch),
    1+z_conversion = exp(integral alpha_void d ell).

Here ell is a declared path length and alpha_void measures a fractional frequency loss per unit length. Positive alpha produces redshift; its size must be derived or fitted. This equation expresses the desired accumulation but does not derive it from a different clock rate. Merely renaming alpha as a time effect would leave the physical cause unexplained.

To make this a theory, specify a clock/propagation field and its interaction, calculate whether the change survives passage back into a galaxy, and account for energy and momentum transferred to companions or other receivers. If all clocks and light respond identically, an apparent coordinate change may cancel from actual clock comparisons. If the environment changes during the journey, include its own energy and evolution. Prior matched-wave/atomic-clock results are relevant constraints on particular couplings, not a rejection of all void-time mechanisms.

## Why this matters and next calculation

If the effect reverses on exit, longer travel cannot explain cumulative redshift. If only photon frequencies change while successive event features keep the same spacing, event-duration stretching remains unexplained. If energy only looks different because different observers use different clock rates, it cannot automatically be counted as energy deposited in a reservoir.

The next conceptual calculation should compare a static spatial clock profile with an evolving propagation field and an explicitly dissipative conversion law, using the same source and detector clock standards. Track both carrier frequency and the separation of signal features, and identify an energy receiver for any irreversible transfer. Keep these as optional candidate comparisons until one is derived and tested; do not silently adopt a new physical law.
