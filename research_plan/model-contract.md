## 2. The constraint within the tested family

Three results combine:
- the clock–ruler family ([clock-ruler-completion](../research_work/results/clock-ruler-completion/report.md));
- the tested matter couplings ([matched-wave](../research_work/results/matched-wave/derivation.md) §4, [shared interaction test](../shared_interaction_test/shared_interaction_report.md), [matter-clock-closure](../research_work/results/matter-clock-closure/report.md));
- PF-1's equivalence with coasting FLRW.

**Within the homogeneous universal-metric family tested**, a redshift that survives comparison with local atoms, with every clock ratio fixed, requires the number of atomic rulers between galaxies to grow. Couplings in that family that avoid this either change clock ratios or local light speed, which precision measurements exclude, or cancel the redshift. BRIDGE-1B puts a number on it: with fixed rulers and a fixed Coulomb fine-structure constant the hyperfine-to-optical ratio drifts at exactly 2p·ṅ/n, where p is the surviving fraction of the coordinate shift, so the Yb⁺/Cs limit allows p ≤ 6.6×10⁻⁷ ([report](../research_work/results/shared-field-bridge/report.md)).

**Co-scaling is one completion compatible with the kinematics of the tested family. Other completions have not been exhaustively classified.** Families not classified include:
- inhomogeneous or screened fields (the [void-screening](../research_work/results/void-screening/report.md) report found a lensing-sign problem in one version);
- non-universal couplings;
- couplings outside a single metric, including frequency- or photon-number-changing processes, which are the original companion mechanism.

## 3. The co-scaling branch (CC-1 results)

CC-1 is a consistency calculation, not a brightness fit. Protocol 6ce4c7a; [report](../research_work/results/clock-completion/report.md).

- **The coupling decides the clock factor.** In 1+z = (n_o/n_e)·ν_atom(t_o)/ν_atom(t_e), the clock factor is derived from the atoms' own dynamics in g_m. Classical and quantum atoms tick at a constant rate in t, to 5×10⁻⁸ and 2×10⁻⁸. The conformal frame puts the whole shift into the clocks instead. Both frames give 1+z = n_o/n_e to 7×10⁻⁹, so nothing is counted twice.
- **Local measurements.**
  - Clock ratios are constant.
  - Light speed in local units is c.
  - A cavity's round trip is 2ℓ/c to 3×10⁻¹⁵ while n doubles.
  - Pulse durations stretch by 1+z.
- **Gravitational waves.**
  - They arrive with the light under either law, because both laws share the metric's light cones.
  - As standard tensor waves of g_m, their amplitude falls as 1/n, giving a siren distance equal to D_L.
  - Under PF-1's postulate P2, the amplitude does not fall, giving D_L/(1+z).
- **Energy ledger.** The field gains exactly what radiation and free motion lose. For the homogeneous toy, energy is conserved to 2×10⁻¹⁴ and the ledger closes to 9×10⁻¹⁵. Bound systems exchange nothing on average. Rest mass exchanges nothing.
- **History, derived rather than imported.**
  - The distance history follows from n(t), which the field's dynamics must supply. Shrinking rulers select no particular history, and the flat-FLRW distances with H0 = 70 and Ωm = 0.3 used in RPG-1's post-hoc diagnostic are an adopted comparison, not a consequence.
  - With V = 0, the declared dynamics turn around at a finite n_min with z_max = E_field/E_radiation. This is a property of that toy, not an established nonsingular past. A potential falling faster than −W/n as n → 0 would remove the turnaround. In atomic units the turnaround is as hot and dense as the corresponding epoch of a hot early universe.
  - A turnaround at least as hot as recombination needs E_field ≥ 1100 E_radiation. That makes today's history coasting (|q₀| ≤ 5×10⁻⁴), whose D_L(z) is the one PF-1 scored at Δχ² = +35.
  - If the field's energy gravitates, the implied density is at least 5.5% of 3H²c²/8πG for recombination, and 1.5×10⁴ times it for nucleosynthesis.
- **What CC-1 does not settle.** It gives no complete gravitational action, no history other than the V = 0 toy, no reservoir supply, and no claim about the original nonexpanding premise, which co-scaling does not meet: separations counted in atomic rulers grow.

## 4. The unification goal (a goal, not a result)

**Goal.** One field sets the redshift, the galaxy-scale gravity and the reservoir, with shared parameters. There is no complete action yet, and the parts tested so far do not connect:
- **Galaxy gravity.** [CG-0](../research_work/results/clock-gradient/report.md) shows one scalar cannot serve as both the rolling frame and the galaxy force. The frame's normalization X = (χ̇/c)² − |∇χ|² crosses zero at 8.5 a*, just above the transition. A completion needs one of:
  - an independent timelike vector, with the AeST structure (Skordis & Złośnik, arXiv:2007.00082);
  - a bi-potential construction sourced through the Newtonian field.

  Its interpolating function must also meet the Solar System bound; the simple ν does not.
- **Lensing.** It needs a derived coupling to the light cone. RPG-1's post-hoc diagnostic compared FLRW-geometry lens predictions with archived dynamical masses fitted in a different, regular-optics geometry. That comparison is a reason for a joint fit, not evidence that lensing and dynamics agree.
- **The reservoir.** CR-1's failures stand with their stated scope.
  - CR-2's supported condensate, with one shared scale of about 80 kpc, fits the lenses worse than free halos and misses the Milky Way threshold.
  - Its supply from local starlight fails by 10⁶–10⁹.
  - If reservoirs exist, they need a cosmological supply (CC-2). In the Milky Way they also need an interior more concentrated than a Thomas–Fermi core with the lenses' scale: the core is too uniform across 5–25 kpc, and its edge plays no role (CR-3, after CF-1).
- **The radiation state s.** Whether matter or radiation controls the local field state must come from the coupling equations. The ratio of baryon to starlight energy density alone does not establish it.

**Provenance.** Ingredients exist in published work:
- static-frame cosmology (Wetterich, arXiv:1303.6878), whose representation is a field redefinition that changes no dimensionless ratio, so its statements about singularities do not transfer without the dynamics;
- k-essence and superfluid dark matter (Berezhiani & Khoury, arXiv:1507.01019);
- TeVeS/AeST lensing;
- rolling condensates.

## 5. Experiment queue (each needs its own declared protocol)

**Premise, after the owner's review of 299ba1f.** No new decision is needed about a "dark-matter-like" medium.
- The [universe contract](universe-contract.md) prohibits an independently inserted halo or bath.
- It permits a gravitating companion population derived from the permitted energy supply and interactions, with its abundance, energy, motion and gravity calculated.
- Such a population would still be an additional gravitating component. Its explained origin and linked predictions are what would distinguish it, not its name.
- CC-1's clock field belongs to the co-scaling branch, so a CC-1-based source is only a labeled comparison. The active objective is a source compatible with operational nonexpansion.

1. **CC-2, cosmological supply.** Given a declared source and interaction, what traveling population is produced, and how does extracting that energy change the source's evolution?
   In stages:
   1. **Stage 1:** a regime and supply audit of CF-1's bath. Done: at CF-1's seeded-only normalization the bath would be opaque, and as a uniform environment heavy. The seedless channel makes that normalization non-authoritative.

      - The companions form an outer envelope. An interior dominated by them needs a total confined mass 16–670 times the baryons.
      - The result is seed-independent, and self-gravity matters.
   3. **A source-side radiation budget and spectrum check,** before 2B selects an interaction (owner's direction after 2c9a110). Done: [RC-1](../research_work/results/radiation-budget/report.md).
      - Today's radiation, converted where it is, falls 10⁵–10⁸ short of the local bath's standing energy (a local stock bound, not a source budget), and FIRAS forbids the non-Planck transfer laws at that scale.
      - Only a Planck-preserving history remains: a 50–340 K past, 38–63 Gyr of transfer, and photon removal about 74 times weaker on starlight than on microwaves.

   4. **Stage 2B-F1: a source and phase-space test** (owner's review of 68eb17c). A coherent field decays into companion pairs; pair production also meets RC-1's momentum objection. Done ([report](../research_work/results/companion-source/report.md)).

It is scored on energy supply and on a hard profile gate: the Milky Way's required M(<r) across 5–25 kpc, without a total confined mass beyond a declared multiple of the baryons. More supply with stage 2A's phase space only builds a heavier envelope.

      - The owner's review of ed96b00 keeps cold infall as the promising ingredient for the inner profile: it holds without the bath's gravity. The net cost is a mass contrast, not a small budget.

   5. **RC-2, redefined as a time-dependent donor-and-companion calculation** (owner's review of ed96b00; the highest priority), for the open, three-sphere and three-torus backgrounds of item 2, an open region first.

      - The computational boundary is kept apart from the observational apertures. Enlarging the region must leave the 5–25 kpc profile and the outer apertures converged, not chosen by R_b.

The baryons may trigger conversion; the energy comes from a separately counted reservoir or a residual parent whose distribution is derived.
   7. **Three-dimensional stability and a common field frame** for a passing collisionless case.
   8. **Finite source histories** before Bose-enhanced decay, which waits until its microphysics is specified. The microwave background does not supply an unspecified reservoir.
   9. **BRIDGE-1B, the shared interaction** (protocol 31d143e). Done ([report](../research_work/results/shared-field-bridge/report.md)). The propagation field produces the companions and feels their back-reaction. The source rate is q = N g ṅ, tied to the redshift rate; the products are cold by mass growth; the radiation supplies 1.5×10⁻⁶ of their rest energy unless the history begins at a 10⁷–10⁹ K turnaround; the brightness stays within Δχ² = 1 of PF-1's only if the field carries 145 times the companions' energy; and in the minimal coupling family the measured shift obeys p ≤ 6.6×10⁻⁷. Stage 2 saturates the mass: the drain becomes a pulse and the brightness matches the coasting law for any crossing beyond the supernovae, so that 145 is a property of indefinite growth, not of the bridge.

10. **PM-1, path-memory gravity** (protocol dcddb70). Done ([report](../research_work/results/path-memory/report.md)). A memory of density alone is inert; a broad wave spectrum with the amplitude growing as √M reaches within 1.6–10.2% of simple MOND's fit on SPARC (and is much worse on one Milky Way fiducial), with an acceleration scale that is the fitted amplitude re-expressed rather than a second prediction; the linear sourcing rule fails the baryonic mass–speed slope; and a receiver whose response carries a memory is unstable for every positive memory time by Routh–Hurwitz. The fitted law is not produced by independent ring saturation, which is representation dependent by √N, but it is the spherical limit of a collective nonlinear field equation. Correction 7: the mass used is force-equivalent (R²g_N/G), so what was fitted is identically the local law g = g_N + √(a\*g_N), and the baryonic mass–speed test is still owed. The spectrum, its confinement and its energy supply remain assumed. PM-2A (`path-memory/protocol-pm2a.md`) separates the four masses and solves two shared-field completions that match that spherical law, comparing their disk and vertical-force predictions. Its stage A is done ([report](../research_work/results/path-memory/report-pm2a.md)): one frozen source per galaxy, independent of the speeds being fitted and integrated in closed form, passing all four gates, with a positive control showing the row-removal test can see a rebuilt source. The measurement it exists to make is that M_force/M_cyl at the last sampled radius has median 1.381 and exceeds one at every galaxy, so the gap between a force-equivalent mass and a source integral is the size the field stages must account for. Stage B then implements four equations — Newtonian, both completions and the simple-μ comparison — each with its own spherical inverse, relaxation weight and field functional, verified on a Plummer sphere to 4.4×10⁻⁷ on the flux identity and 2.3×10⁻⁷ against g_N + √(a\*g_N), with the completions agreeing where they must and simple-μ distinct at 1.618034 where it must be. 11. **PM-3, the scope of a shared-field memory** (protocol 9f57c99). Done ([report](../research_work/results/path-memory/report-pm3.md)). The owner's bounded-memory toy reproduces: disturbed orbits settle, the energy ledger closes exactly, and Routh–Hurwitz reduces to b > 0, so every positive finite memory time is stable — candidate C's instability was a property of its constitutive law, not a theorem against delayed response. But any relaxational memory is equivalent to a static law of the same instantaneous fields in a steady state, so memory buys transients and not support: in the toy the settled orbit's memory contribution is exactly zero and the flat-curve term is assumed, and the proposed field memory returns exactly to Completion I for any stationary source, making it blind to steady rotation at every τ and η. Steady rotation-dependent gravity must therefore come from a coupling to a mass current or velocity stress, or from the discreteness of stars — declared as stage 3 and not run.

12. **RUT-1, the written-track model** (protocol 390bc37). Stages 0–4 done ([stages 0–2](../research_work/results/path-memory/report-rut1.md), [stage 3](../research_work/results/path-memory/report-rut3.md), [stage 4](../research_work/results/path-memory/report-rut4.md), [stage 4R](../research_work/results/path-memory/report-rut4r.md)). A body writes a fading footprint along its own path; a steady writing pattern leaves Φ = −τ·(pattern), so PM-3's theorem does not cover it. The track is genuinely attractive, vertically confining and felt by a second body, and the mature-ring control reproduces its declared numbers. But a family of self-written tracks scales as 1/R², not 1/R; and on a prescribed orbit the support accumulates with the memory time while the mature drag approaches a retention-independent limit, so their ratio is C(w/R)/N_build with C in closed form. Holding the orbit to one retention time at 10% mature support costs driver angular momentum of 9.81 to 0.98 times the body's own across w/R = 0.05 to 0.4: a real support–torque trade-off for a lone writer, though the associated width inequality is a timescale crossing rather than a survival theorem. Several distinct writers sharing one track at fixed total writing rate largely escape it, keeping 99.93% of the support at 2.4% of the drag at sixteen writers, with the coincident-copy control passing at 2.2×10⁻¹⁶. Stage 3 then evolved bodies and field together from an empty field: the lone writer is destroyed on the schedule the prescribed map predicted, sixteen writers survive 20 orbits keeping 80% of their angular momentum, but a 2% perturbation breaks the ring up while the same disturbed start without memory stays bounded. The obstacle is continued writing rather than formation, since a body launched in equilibrium with an already-built track also collapses. Two of those stage 3 claims were then withdrawn: the ejections were numerical, since a fixed timestep mishandles close passages while a circular no-memory control cannot detect it, and the attribution to "continued writing" was replaced by split primed controls showing that a frozen field and a decaying field with no new writing both leave the angular momentum exactly unchanged. The owner's two-stage candidate — tau_form dE/dt = S - E with dC/dt = E - C/tau_keep, the one-stage model being exactly tau_form = 0 and sharing its steady state — then wins every matched pair, retaining 97.5 to 99.6% of angular momentum against 71 to 86% and showing positive measured support where the baseline is negative. Stage 4 then ran the longer horizon: twenty declared runs to 100 reference periods with the equation frozen. The one-stage model fails in every configuration, including two that had completed twenty periods, so a twenty-period result was not a result. The two-stage response survives everywhere with 9-14% measured support, but the radial motion left in it is a large-scale instability: from a cold mature ring the m = 2 band grows with an amplitude e-folding time near nine periods and, at saturation, carries 89% of the angular-momentum loss, while fine structure at the writer spacing stays five to eight orders down. A declared grid-rotation control and an independent spectral implementation both reproduce it. Stage 4R gives the same response a reciprocal form whose matter-plus-field energy closes against a derived dissipation rate to 3.4e-7. Stage 5 then supplied the control stage 4 lacked, at the owner's direction: a zero-delay field with the same kernel and the same static gain is m = 2 unstable with an e-folding of 0.19 periods, and ordinary pairwise attraction at matched support with 0.13, against the two-stage model's 8.58 and the one-stage model's 4.45. Collective attraction destabilises a cold supported ring on a fraction of an orbit, and the lag is what slows it; the attribution of the instability to the two-stage response is withdrawn. A linear mode analysis of the exact-history model now predicts stage 4's measured growth (8.579 against 8.47-8.61 under four-fold refinement) and gives the pattern speed that was missing: the mode is nearly stationary in the inertial frame. Growth rises with support across every sampled strength, is unchanged from 32 to 128 writers, and is independent of the initial disturbance, which sets only when the mode becomes visible; no spectrum is claimed complete, since the contour root count is itself unreliable and is repaired in stage 6. Part of what stage 4 reported as heating was coherent two-lobed streaming counted as dispersion. Stage 6 part 1 then made the two repairs the owner named as preconditions for any stability claim, and constructed the state to be tested. A contour-integral eigensolver validated against three exactly known spectra now locates every root in a declared region, and shows the two-stage cold ring has exactly one unstable mode there; the seeded verification, repaired to perturb the fields as well as the bodies, reproduces the predicted mode to 0.014%. An orbital population is constructed in exact equilibrium with the field it writes, as a distribution function of the conserved quantities, across narrow and wide and cold and warm annuli, and is stationary under the simulator when the field is frozen. The declared stationary-control gate failed and is recorded as failed. That last reading did not survive review: the sampler drew a mis-weighted population, the 10% support label was a grid artefact (the mass-weighted support was 13% and 20%), and both archived annulus runs carry a growing, inertially stationary m = 2 mode with an e-folding of 5 to 9 periods that azimuthally averaged statistics could not see, so the warm annulus was not quiet. The milestone remains unreached; part 2, the mode analysis around those populations, is declared and not run. Support, stability and settling remain separate claims: this equation buys the first, delays the loss of the second, and has not produced the third.

## 6. What would stop each line early

- **Co-scaling.** A declared field dynamics that reaches the recombination and nucleosynthesis temperatures cannot fit the brightness at equal freedom, or requires field energy that gravitating tests exclude.
- **CG-1.** No vector or bi-potential completion gives the radial-acceleration relation, lensing equal to dynamics at low acceleration, the Milky Way vertical force and the Solar System bound together.
