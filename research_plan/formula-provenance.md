# Standing rule: identify formula provenance

**Persistent-background extension:** [Source-labeled formulas](persistent-microwave-background.md) distinguish established S^3 eigenmodes, oscillator energy balance, angular-spectrum definitions and the Planck distribution from our proposed field/source/transfer identification. The combined construction is a hypothesis; mathematical novelty and empirical success are not claimed.

User instruction, 9 September 2026: whenever presenting formulas, indicate whether they are unique to this work or restatements of known literature.

Every displayed formula in future conversation, papers and reports must carry a nearby provenance label and enough explanation to distinguish:

- **Established mathematics or physics**: a known identity, standard law or generic mathematical solution. Cite an appropriate primary source when attributing a specific literature result; never imply novelty merely from changed notation.
- **Adapted from prior work**: identify the source and the actual change of assumptions, variables or application.
- **Derived here within a proposed model; originality unverified**: explain the starting assumptions and derivation. Deriving something in this project does not establish that nobody derived it before.
- **Proposed here; originality unverified**: a new assumption or candidate law in this project, not yet derived or established as original in the literature.
- **Empirically calibrated here**: a fitted parameter or relationship; distinguish new numerical estimates from the established mathematical form.

Use an originality claim only after an explicit prior-art review supports it, and qualify the scope of that review. If provenance is uncertain, say so rather than guessing. A section-level label can cover several equations only when its scope is unambiguous. This applies to simple equations as well as the central mechanism.

## Current examples and retrospective clarification

The exponential integral for accumulated fractional redshift is established mathematics and appears in prior redshift models; it is not our discovery. Its constant numerical rate is calibrated here on an exposed catalog.

The optical Hamiltonian, effective-index ray equations and energy-conservation methods are established structures. Their application to the proposed companion field is a candidate construction, not an established novel theory.

The finite-radiation homogeneous-field result was derived and numerically checked here under stated assumptions. Its originality is unverified. Neither the inverse-index photon energy nor the resulting integrable mechanical equation should be called unique without a literature review.

Next manuscript revision must apply these labels to all equations, distinguish literature sources from internal derivations, and preserve uncertainty about the proposed interaction's originality. Existing manuscript v0.2 has not yet undergone that complete provenance revision.
