# Decision: use already emitted radiation for Earth conversion

User request: identify electrons/EM energy losses and choose a defensible source for companion conversion. Working decision: apply the existing conversion law to outgoing terrestrial radiation after emission. Do not assume continuous energy extraction from stable ground-state atoms or add a new percentage drain to Earth's interior. This is a hypothesis choice, not an observational upper bound on allowable conversion.

## What supplies energy

Earth emits thermal infrared radiation. Electronic, molecular and lattice processes can exchange and radiate excitation energy; QED describes electromagnetic interactions, not a new net power supply. An atom in its ground state cannot continuously emit by falling to a lower state. Excited-state emission is paid for by excitation energy ([Feynman, stationary states](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/III_07.html)). Virtual-photon exchange is not a separately extractable luminosity.

The Earth system absorbs roughly 240 W/m^2 of sunlight averaged over its surface ([NASA energy budget](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/climate-and-earths-energy-budget/)). With mean radius 6.371e6 m this is 1.22415e17 W, a useful approximate scale for outgoing thermal radiation, not a precise present net balance. Much terrestrial radiation is reprocessed solar input, not independent fuel supplied by Earth's atoms.

Earth's measured interior surface heat flow is approximately 47+/-2 TW ([Davies and Davies 2010](https://se.copernicus.org/articles/1/5/2010/)). It is a throughput with an existing physical role, not an unexplained surplus available to divert. It ultimately contributes to emission and must not be counted again on top of a radiation budget containing it. Radioactivity, cooling and chemical or electrical activity release finite stored/supplied energy; they do not give unlimited electron energy.

## Conversion budget with the retained rate

Use the **existing postulated conversion law**, not a new fitted efficiency:

    P_companion(D)=P_IR*[1-exp(-alpha*D)],
    alpha=0.0002488993286382367/Mpc.

For outgoing terrestrial radiation traveling from the surface to the lunar orbital radius, D=384400000-6371000=378029000 m. The fraction is 3.04929e-18 and the converted power is approximately **0.37328 W**, summed over all outgoing directions. It is not power incident on or captured by the Moon. Perfect retention of all this converted energy somewhere inside that sphere would add at most **1.31068e-10 kg/year** of deposited mass-equivalent under E/c^2. Real retention requires a capture calculation and is no larger under this source-only accounting.

This transfer occurs after Earth has emitted the photons, so it adds no new source-side cooling in the assumed transport branch. It reduces photon energy in transit. At most 0.37328 W goes to companions on these paths under the retained coefficient, rather than choosing an unsupported 1% or 10% of Earth's thermal output. This is a prediction/extrapolation, not a measured or experimentally certified permitted loss. Interplanetary propagation, capture, spectroscopy and thermal balance still constrain the branch.

## Does an existing well consume power?

Known GR treats matter's existing stress-energy as the source of curvature; it does not require a continuously dissipating power supply to maintain a static well ([Einstein Online](https://www.einstein-online.info/en/spotlight/gravity_of_gravity/)). Existing rest energy is a contribution, not energy free to spend again. A static electric field likewise does not imply a continual emitted luminosity.

If our hypothetical well must be a separately funded reservoir, make its storage/decay assumption explicit. **Hypothetical reservoir law using conservation accounting:**

    dot E_well=P_capture-E_well/tau,
    P_required=E_well/tau  (steady state).

tau is a new physical lifetime, not known from the strength of gravity. Permanent storage is tau->infinity and requires no replenishment solely to offset decay; its formation still requires a source. Finite tau needs a specified return/escape channel for the lost energy. This optional decay equation does not silently replace our retained permanent-deposit branch.

The weak-field source identification M_extra=E_well/c^2 is a conditional effective-mass relation, not a universal local energy density for the gravitational field. It must not be confused with a body's gravitational binding energy. Any hypothesis replacing the entire ordinary mass source needs a new field/source derivation; the 0.373-W budget does not accomplish that. No energy surplus capable of explaining the entire terrestrial well has been identified here.

This decision supplies a funded small conversion channel while preserving atomic stability and explicit energy bookkeeping. It neither claims a microscopic QED-to-graviton process nor resolves all six research goals.
