# Photon energy to graviton energy: what is known and what must be derived

We do not yet know a physical conversion rate for this candidate. Four quantities must be kept distinct.

## 1. Energy accounting

If photon energy decreases by a positive amount Delta E_gamma, conservation requires that the complete system account for it:

```
Delta E_gamma = E_companions + Delta E_driver + E_recoil + E_heat + ...
```

The receiving terms are defined without counting interaction energy twice; some field changes can be signed. If a driver supplies additional energy, the outgoing companion energy can exceed the photon loss only by explicitly debiting that driver.

The optional full-transfer, no-secondary-loss branch assumes all photon loss enters traveling companions and that they retain it until capture. Within that branch:

```
E_companions = Delta E_gamma
```

One joule lost by photons supplies one joule to companions. This equality is the stipulated destination of the energy, not a prediction of how rapidly conversion occurs.

## 2. Energy inferred from a frequency change

For photon energy E=h_P f, using a consistent emission/detection energy standard:

```
Delta E_gamma = h_P (f_emitted - f_received)
```

If the intrinsic source frequency and receiver standards justify f_received=f_emitted/(1+z), then the inferred lost fraction is z/(1+z). At z=1, half the initial photon energy remains and half is available for transfer under this interpretation. The chosen interaction must also reproduce photon counts and arrival times before this per-photon statement becomes an observed luminosity law.

A spectral shift alone does not identify its energy recipient. Matter-clock changes, drivers and the propagation mechanism must be treated together. We do not infer confirmed graviton production from redshift.

## 3. Production rate and graviton number

The model must derive a loss coefficient and an energy-partition fraction from its interaction and environment. For a stipulated local law dE_gamma/ds=-kappa_loss E_gamma, full transfer would give dE_companions/ds=kappa_loss E_gamma before secondary effects. A fitted kappa_loss is an empirical parameter until an action predicts it.

There need not be one graviton per photon. If the companion is quantized ordinary massless tensor radiation with energies h_P f_i, the total emitted energy is sum_i h_P f_i. This does not determine the number or frequencies separately. Other companion identities need their own dispersion law. Momentum, angular momentum, allowed channels and reverse processes also constrain production.

## 4. Capture and gravity

Capture efficiency determines how much produced energy stays in a chosen well. A gravitational-response factor determines how the stored energy affects motion and lensing. Neither is the photon-to-companion energy exchange rate, and neither may create unaccounted energy.

The immediate research task is to calculate a transition or wave interaction that predicts the photon frequency/number changes, companion spectrum, receiving-sector transfers and capture behavior together. The [universe contract](universe-contract.md) requires that this use fixed published galaxy distances and no assumed dark-matter, expansion or Big-Bang source model.
