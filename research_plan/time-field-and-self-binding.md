# Time-field formula, companion self-binding, and the expansion comparison

This note records the user's questions and possible companion-binding extension. The fictional universe remains nonexpanding, with the same adopted distances and observations as before. Standard expansion is discussed only as a comparison, not inserted into our model.

## Could gravitons attract one another like an atomic bond?

Self-attraction is a relevant possibility to investigate. Gravitational-wave energy sourcing the gravity that confines the waves has been studied in [Brill and Hartle's gravitational-geon calculation](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.135.B271). A separate [massive-graviton study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05606) finds localized states in a specific bigravity theory and discusses instability and decay. These are theoretical constructions, not observed atomic-style graviton bonds or an established solution for our proposed halos. The latter study's particle mass, gravity theory and dark-matter application are not adopted here.

For our purposes, attraction would need to do more than bend a passing wave's path. It must admit a sufficiently long-lived bound state, explain how an incoming companion enters it, account for the binding energy released, and predict its spatial extent. It must also explain why stored energy occupies galaxy outskirts rather than escaping, falling inward or concentrating too tightly. The resulting field must affect stellar motion and lensing consistently.

A bound state's mass/energy cannot be obtained by adding an unexplained gravity bonus. If its energy is below that of the incoming free constituents, the difference must go into an accounted radiation, recoil, internal-excitation or other channel. Any interaction added to provide attraction also contributes energy and stress.

The current scalar companion simulation includes none of this gravitational self-binding. It is not a calculation of ordinary spin-2 gravitons. Record self-binding as an open research branch; do not silently adopt a bond law or claim that the free-field spherical test excludes it.

## What mathematical rule represents the proposed time effect?

We currently test an effective propagation-clock field, not a completed fundamental definition of time. With s the fixed reference path length, t the declared reference time, c0 the reference light speed and n a dimensionless optical/time factor,

```
dt/ds = n(s,t)/c0,
n(s,t) = 1 + g(s) phi(s,t)                 [local shorthand],
omega = c0 k/n.
```

Here phi is the candidate companion/time field and g says where and how strongly it affects propagation. The actual numerical n is a Gaussian spatial average of g phi. The desired connection between g and weak gravity is still to be derived. Source/detector clock laws are also still to be derived; the current comparisons use explicitly common reference standards.

Hamilton's equations give, along the photon,

```
dE_gamma/dt = -E_gamma (partial_t n)/n,
alpha(s,t) = (partial_t n)/c0,
S = exp[integral_along_ray alpha ds],
E_received = E_emitted/S,
net companion gain from this photon = E_emitted (1-1/S).
```

All actual photon energy lost enters the companion field. This is generated by the shared photon/field Hamiltonian, whose field-work term is the negative of the photon-energy change. It is not an efficiency chosen after calculating the redshift. The Hamiltonian itself remains a candidate postulate; deriving its consequences does not establish it as a fundamental law of nature.

With the same local propagation conditions at emission and reception, the observed wavelength ratio is S. The derivative of event arrival time with respect to emission time is also S. One common finite-event stretch follows only when S is effectively constant across that event. Otherwise different portions can stretch differently.

Within a changing medium, spatial wavelength and oscillation period need not change identically because the propagation speed is changing too. The wavelength-ratio statement above compares the source and detector under matched conditions. It must not be interpreted as proof that spatial crests separate in precisely the same way everywhere inside the region.

For example, S=2 means a received photon has half its emitted energy, the other half has entered companions, and a locally undistorted event has twice its duration. The amount of arrival delay is a different quantity from this duration stretch.

For this particular formula, a fixed spatial n(s) gives delay but partial_t n=0 and no ongoing frequency stretching between matched endpoint standards. The [spherical propagation result](../research_work/results/spherical-propagation/report.md) shows approach toward that situation under a steady compact source. This does not undo the redshift of photons that crossed while the field was changing. It limits new redshift generated after settling. A static bound companion store would face the same distinction unless its interaction also maintains an evolving propagation clock.

## Would expansion face the same tests?

Both models must predict observed wavelengths, event durations, brightness and spectra. In the standard expanding metric, the wavelength and local arrival-duration ratios are a_received/a_emitted; a is the spatial scale factor. Its geometric evolution already supplies the time-dependent propagation. Our model must derive and maintain its n field instead. The scale-factor relation is also stated in [Hogg's distance-measures review](https://ned.ipac.caltech.edu/level5/Hogg/Hogg3.html).

The similarity can be made explicit: radial light in expanding-space coordinates obeys dt/dchi=a(t)/c0. Setting n=a gives the same form as our propagation equation, and integrating its stretch gives a_received/a_emitted. Here chi is a comoving coordinate, whereas our s is fixed reference distance; the matching equation does not make the physical rulers, clock laws or energy dynamics identical. See the light-ray derivation in [Tong's cosmology notes, section 1.1.3](https://davidtong.org/teaching/cosmology/cosmohtml/S1).

Standard general relativity uses local stress-energy conservation, with the cosmological form d(rho a^3)=-p d(a^3), where rho is energy density. This is not a claim that each lost photon-energy unit appears as a captured graviton. Our explicit photon-to-companion ledger is an additional physical requirement. Neither model is exempt from its own conservation equations or observational tests. In a general evolving spacetime, local conservation need not provide a globally conserved energy total of the ordinary fixed-background form; the symmetry conditions and their limits are discussed in [Tong's general-relativity notes, sections 4.3.2 and 4.5.5](https://davidtong.org/pdfs/teaching/general-relativity/gr.pdf). This does not authorize unaccounted energy in our explicitly closed photon/companion calculations.

The spherical result concerns a particular field sourced by localized radiation. It is not a theorem that cumulative time-based stretching cannot reproduce expansion-like observations. Distinct underlying causes can give matching propagation relations; the shared dynamics, source history, matter response and other observations must distinguish them.

## Next concrete requirements for the proposed binding branch

1. State whether the interaction is ordinary gravitational self-interaction or a new companion force, and specify fields, propagation and coupling constants. Without this, “attraction” does not predict an outcome.
2. Derive binding energies, stable sizes and lifetimes from the same action. Without this, a temporary concentration could be mistaken for a durable deposit.
3. Calculate entry and energy-release channels. Without them, a mathematically bound state may never be populated by incoming companions.
4. Predict how the bound and traveling components affect partial_t n, including reverse energy transfer. Without this, improved storage may still leave the redshift mechanism inactive.
5. Join the resulting stress-energy to both orbital motion and lensing, including extended halo support and available photon supply. Without this, an attractive state need not provide the required galaxy-scale gravity.

These requirements supplement the existing 20 tasks and 32 observational areas. They do not replace distributed-illumination, clock, energy-supply or validation work, and no binding mechanism is adopted by this note.
