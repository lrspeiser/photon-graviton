# Theory first: photon–companion gravity

**User-requested research framing, 21 September 2026.** Begin each research discussion with the physical hypothesis, connect the selected calculation to that hypothesis, and identify the mechanism or equation being solved for. This guide is an intended physical architecture, not a claim of a completed or observationally established theory. It does not change archived equations, results, branch declarations, or negative findings.

## The opening we reuse

Our working hypothesis is that matter, light, and gravity participate in a connected, energy-conserving physical system. Light and matter can exchange energy with a companion field; that field may propagate, retain history, and form an extended gravitational structure around galaxies. The structure must supply the additional inward pull inferred from galaxy orbits and bend passing light through the same derived physical response. A whole solar system may follow that galactic field while its planets retain ordinary orbits around their star, because the broad external pull is nearly shared and the additional local response is suppressed or negligible. We are solving for the production, spatial organization, lifetime, matter/light coupling, and nested-system behavior that make this one mechanism rather than separate formulas.

The words **may**, **must**, and **solving for** matter: this is the hypothesis and its required consequences, not a list of completed achievements.

## The physical story

Imagine pulling back from the Sun and its planets until the entire solar system becomes a point in the Galaxy. There are now two distinct motions to explain: planets orbiting their star, and that star with its planets orbiting the Galaxy. A theory must explain both simultaneously.

The project's wider proposal is that the space around a galaxy is not described only by a snapshot of its currently visible matter. Matter and radiation can excite a companion sector. Traveling companions may exchange energy with wells and form extended bound configurations, while a field's evolving state may retain information about past sources and motions. Such memory is physical state with a formation process, finite capacity, transport, and possible decay—not a record that generates energy for free.

In the provisional Phase Junction integration, the photon, companion, and tensor graviton are different modes, not different names for the same particle. The proposed companion is a neutral relative-phase excitation; a deposit is a bound collective configuration of that same sector. Their shared microscopic origin and complete large-scale behavior are not yet derived.

The distinctive aim is to derive the extra gravitational structure from a universal source-and-evolution rule. We do not assign an independent invisible halo to each galaxy. Nevertheless, stored companion energy would be additional unseen gravitational content; the proposal is about its physical origin and behavior, not a claim that no additional field state exists.

### Why outer stars can remain fast

The relevant observation is not that every star gets continually faster at larger radius. Many rotation curves rise and then become approximately flat: outer material moves faster than the visible-matter-only prediction. These are different objects at different radii, not automatically one object gaining speed as it moves outward.

The proposed extended response supplies inward acceleration across the outskirts. It need not become stronger in absolute terms with distance; it can weaken more slowly than the ordinary field of a compact source. A circular orbit needs inward acceleration v^2/r. If the relevant outer acceleration falls approximately as 1/r over a finite galactic range, the circular speed is approximately constant. Doubling the orbit's radius then halves the required inward pull rather than quartering it.

This is additional steering, not a tangential engine pushing a star faster on every lap. Any actual change in orbital energy, capture, or circularization must be accounted for by exchange with the evolving field and its sources. A static inward force alone does not circularize arbitrary orbits.

The square-root low-acceleration formula is one mathematical target with this outer behavior. It is not the full mechanism and is not an established unique result of the junction algebra. Real disks require the field of the actual source geometry, not a spherical formula relabeled as a disk solution.

### Why the same structure can lens light

Now place a distant galaxy behind the foreground galaxy. Its light passes through the extended gravitational structure. In a geometric implementation, that structure changes the geometry through which photons travel; a bundle of rays can be redirected, stretched, focused, or split into multiple images. Light need not be assigned a rest mass for this to happen.

The core requirement is one derived physical state with connected matter and photon responses. We cannot choose one extra-gravity multiplier for stellar motion and a different freely fitted one for lensing. In a weak-field description, the time and spatial gravitational responses both contribute to light deflection, whereas slow-body motion mostly probes the time response. Their relationship must be derived. A force law for stars does not automatically provide a lensing theory.

Photon energy transfer and light deflection are also different effects: changing a photon's frequency is not the same as bending its direction. The common theory must supply both processes where it claims both; one cannot stand in for the other. A nonmetric branch remains possible but must supply its own consistent photon propagation and matter response rather than borrowing a lensing formula without derivation.

### Why this need not disrupt planets

The Galaxy can steer the whole solar system while the Sun steers the planets within it. Suppose the broad galactic field gives the Sun and Earth nearly the same acceleration. In Earth's motion relative to the Sun, that common acceleration cancels; the difference of the two external accelerations is what can disturb their relative orbit. The desired galaxy-scale structure varies little across a planetary system, so that difference should be small.

This cancellation is a conditional consequence of universal, nearly uniform acceleration, not a proof that every nonlinear companion model achieves it. Tidal gradients, nonuniversal coupling, and changes to the Sun's own field remain part of the nested-system solution.

There is a second requirement: the mechanism must not create a large extra Sun–planet attraction locally. A broadly distributed reservoir may have negligible local differential gravity; a modified-response branch may instead require a derived high-acceleration or environmental suppression. These are physically different implementations, not interchangeable claims that the mechanism is already solved.

Do not suppress a star's response to the galactic field merely because its own surface gravity is large. That would remove the very galaxy-orbit effect we seek. Suppress or make negligible the unwanted extra *internal* force while retaining the shared response of the system as a whole.

“Outside a gravity well” is an intuitive description, not yet a microscopic switch. Distance, potential depth, local acceleration, source density, and accumulated history are distinct quantities. Derive which controls production, capture, storage, and response; do not silently replace the user's physical idea with one of them. Capture associated with a well need not produce a compact deposit: a bound configuration can extend far beyond the capturing region, but its extent and support must be solved.

In particular, suppressing an extra gravitational response is not the same as suppressing photon conversion. The historical direct-conversion branch explicitly distinguishes conversion along a path from capture in wells. The later companion ontology leaves its propagation speed to be derived; the older effective assumption that it travels at c is not a completed derivation.

## What we solve for

### 1. The source-to-structure mechanism

Derive what excites the companion, the amount of available energy, the propagation and capture interactions, and an extended supported configuration. Output: a sourced field or reservoir profile with its normalization and energy/momentum accounting, not an independently chosen halo. Long elapsed time alone does not establish an adequate energy supply.

### 2. The nested-system solution

Place a compact star and its planets inside the same extended galaxy field. Solve their common galactic acceleration, internal relative forces, and residual tides from the same coupling. Output: the physical reason for a substantial galaxy-orbit effect and negligible planetary-orbit anomaly—not a hand-applied switch for objects called planets.

### 3. The joint matter-and-light response

Derive the relationship between the source state, massive-body trajectories, photon trajectories, and clock response. Output: stellar dynamics and lensing from the same solution, without separate fitted response multipliers. Separate propagating modes are allowed; independent arbitrary phenomenologies are not a substitute for their coupling.

### 4. Memory, persistence, and energy exchange

Derive what persists when matter moves, how it spreads, how long it lasts, and where energy and angular momentum go. Output: a causal history-dependent field and its response to moving sources. Orbital circularization, merger offsets, and long-lived deposits require this dynamics; they do not follow merely from writing a static force law.

These are construction goals. Verification remains necessary to know whether a proposed solution is correct, but a new checklist is not itself a new physical solution. Each work session should identify the physical link being solved, give its explicit output, and state what that output changes in the overall explanation.

## Boundary between the theory and current candidates

SM-1 is a phenomenological screened total-potential construction, not the whole photon–companion theory and not an identified microscopic companion field. Its numerical checks, isolated-force suppression, and static-orbit results are scoped results. Its lensing coupling, nested external-field solution, and source/history mechanism remain unresolved. Do not begin future discussions by silently treating SM-1 as the user's ontology.

Retain the expanded portfolio: memory, collective response, separate tensor radiation, and other declared alternatives are not excluded merely by the failure of one local scalar or finite regulator. Explain the exact equation and assumptions responsible for a failure, and distinguish an implementation failure from a mechanism-wide obstruction. Conversely, a useful narrative is not evidence that a missing mechanism has been solved.

The wider redshift and persistent microwave-background objectives remain in the active program. No dark-matter, expansion, or Big-Bang premise is adopted as a requirement by this guide, and no success on those wider objectives is claimed.
