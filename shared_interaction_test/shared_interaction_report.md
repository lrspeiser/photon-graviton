# Shared matter–light–gravity interaction and a fixed-law brightness test

This study develops a common action for a candidate temporal propagation model and tests the existing exponential distance law against higher-redshift supernova brightnesses. The fictional universe is required to have no expansion and no particulate dark matter. The candidate remains unscreened. Atomic standards are intended to remain unchanged, but that requirement is tested rather than silently enforced after interactions are introduced.

Two concrete issues emerge. First, a simple shared electromagnetic action preserves leading electrostatic binding only by changing magnetic interactions, so it does not protect all atomic transitions. Ordinary Einstein gravity also fails to sustain the assumed spatially flat static background with the specified positive-energy components. Second, the fixed brightness law predicts the standardized supernovae systematically too bright, with a residual trend across redshift. These results identify necessary extensions; they do not exclude every possible time-based model.

## 1. One common action

Use units c₀=ℏ=1 in the action and metric signature (−,+,+,+). Let g be the metric for matter and the Einstein gravitational term. Let n>0 be the propagation scalar, and u be a dynamical timelike vector constrained by gμνuμuν=−1. A concrete two-metric candidate is

\[
\bar g_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+(1-n^{-2})u_\mu u_\nu,
\]

\[
S=\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{M_{Pl}^2}{2}{\cal R}
-\frac K2 g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu n\partial_\nu n-V(n)
-\frac\beta4 U_{\mu\nu}U^{\mu\nu}
+\lambda(u_\mu u^\mu+1)\right]
+S_{matter}[g,A,\Psi;m_i,e]
-\frac14\int d^4x\sqrt{-\bar g}\,Z(n)\bar g^{\mu\alpha}\bar g^{\nu\beta}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}.
\]

Here F=dA, U=du, K and β are positive, and Z>0 controls the electromagnetic normalization. Matter is minimally coupled through the same gauge potential A that appears in the traveling electromagnetic field; masses and charges are constant in the matter action. This avoids pretending that bound and propagating photons are unrelated species. Protons and nuclei are treated as effective matter degrees of freedom; QCD and nuclear magnetic moments still require a deeper completion.

The constrained vector supplies the preferred frame dynamically rather than inserting an externally fixed direction. This simple vector action is a candidate, not a proven stable completion: longitudinal modes, constraints, strong coupling, and global evolution need analysis. Positive Maxwell and scalar kinetic coefficients alone are insufficient to certify the full theory. The disformal-metric literature provides a framework for studying such couplings, not a proof of viability for this particular action [1].

Gauge invariance gives charge conservation. Varying the full action with respect to g gives

\[
M_{Pl}^2G_{\mu\nu}=T^{matter}_{\mu\nu}+T^n_{\mu\nu}+T^u_{\mu\nu}+T^{EM}_{\mu\nu}.
\]

On all field equations, covariance gives ∇μTtotal μν=0. Individual sectors can exchange energy and momentum. Thus radiation lost energy cannot simply be removed from the gravitational source. The electromagnetic equation can be written as the divergence of √(−ḡ)Z ḡḡF equaling the matter current density, with the same A coupling in both sectors.

## 2. What the shared action does to atoms

In the local matter rest frame, g=diag(−1,1,1,1) and ḡ=diag(−n⁻²,1,1,1). Expanding the electromagnetic term gives

\[
\mathcal L_{EM}=\tfrac12 Zn\,E^2-\tfrac12(Z/n)B^2,
\qquad \epsilon_r=Zn,\quad\mu_r=n/Z,\quad c_\gamma/c_0=1/n.
\]

Fixed masses and charges give leading hydrogen energies proportional to (Zn)⁻² and the Bohr radius proportional to Zn. Keeping both fixed selects Z=1/n, normalized today. Then

\[
\boxed{\epsilon_r=1,\qquad\mu_r=n^2.}
\]

This leaves the leading Coulomb potential and nonrelativistic optical energy scale unchanged. It does **not** leave the magnetic interaction unchanged. With fixed electron and nuclear moments and fixed leading wavefunctions, a magnetic hyperfine interaction scales as μr, giving νhfs∝n². Consequently a leading optical/hyperfine frequency ratio has

\[
\frac{d\ln(\nu_{opt}/\nu_{hfs})}{dt}\simeq-2\frac{\dot n}{n}.
\]

For the fixed astronomical coefficient, γ=7.7315×10⁻¹¹ yr⁻¹ today, this is −1.5463×10⁻¹⁰ yr⁻¹. The simple branch therefore violates its intended exact atomic-invariance requirement internally, even before data are considered. There is no choice of the single function Z that holds both εr and μr constant while n changes, since εrμr=n². This is a restricted constitutive result, not a general no-go theorem for all microscopic theories.

Lange et al. directly report a drift of the Yb⁺ E3/Cs frequency ratio of −3.1(3.4)×10⁻¹⁷ yr⁻¹ [2]. Comparing the direct ratio is preferable here to reusing their inferred fine-structure-constant bound, whose standard sensitivity interpretation does not automatically apply to two different light cones. If the measured ratio drift is written as s_eff γ, the published Gaussian summary gives s_eff between −1.26×10⁻⁶ and +4.61×10⁻⁷ at 1.96 standard deviations. Our leading estimate is s_eff≈−2.

This is a severe scale mismatch, not a precision exclusion significance. Cs and Yb⁺ require many-electron, relativistic, magnetic and nuclear calculations in the new theory before assigning an exact prediction. To preserve this candidate, extra matter couplings would have to protect multiple dimensionless ratios, not merely cancel one measurement after the fact. An added magnetic-moment coupling or a causal dispersive photon sector is retained as a possible extension; neither has been demonstrated here.

## 3. Recovering propagation and energy exchange

Geometric optics of the electromagnetic action gives the null cone of ḡ and the Hamiltonian Hγ=c₀|p|/n used in Appendix D. For Z=1/n and a homogeneous field, the electromagnetic Lagrangian is E²/2−B²/(2n²). Its scalar source is B²/n³. For a traveling wave B²=n²E² in normalized units, this source is uγ/n, reproducing

\[
K\ddot n+V'(n)=u_\gamma/n
\]

in the fixed-background homogeneous limit. The reduced radiation–field energy budget was therefore not arbitrary: it can be recovered from this shared action at that order. Recovering that one limit does not repair its atomic or gravitational problems.

The free-field affine history n=1+γt remains an approximation when radiation exchanges appreciable energy. The higher-redshift test below intentionally retains the original free-law formula unchanged; it does not fit a radiation correction or a new coupling to improve the outcome.

## 4. The nonexpanding background must solve gravity

For a spatially flat homogeneous metric, Einstein's equations require 3MPl²H²=ρtotal and −2MPl²Ḣ=ρtotal+ptotal. A genuinely static solution needs both quantities on the right to vanish. At today's n=1, the electromagnetic metric coincides with the matter metric. For the homogeneous rest-frame solution with U=0, the vector constraint equation gives λ=0 at this epoch. The listed matter, radiation and canonical rolling scalar give

\[
\rho_{total}+p_{total}=\rho_b+\tfrac43\rho_\gamma+K\gamma^2>0.
\]

Scalar potential energy or an ordinary cosmological constant cannot cancel this sum. Thus this simplest common-action branch does not support the assumed flat static universe with ordinary positive-energy matter and radiation. Fixing the metric by hand would omit a field equation, not solve it.

Positive spatial curvature changes the condition. An Einstein-static-type geometry requires ρ+3p=0 and an appropriate curvature radius at the background level. Curvature and a potential may allow an instantaneous balance, but continuing evolution, field–radiation exchange and perturbation stability must all be solved. Existing Einstein-static studies show why stability is a separate question [3]. A sphere remains a candidate, not a demonstrated solution. It would also change the geometric area-distance relation, so adopting it later requires a separately labeled brightness law rather than silently changing the fixed Euclidean test below.

## 5. The action also exposes a gravitational-wave issue

In this minimal Einstein plus Maxwell-type-vector action, tensor waves about the homogeneous background propagate on g at c₀, while photons propagate on ḡ at c₀/n. The cones coincide today but differed along a long path. For simultaneous emission at fixed geometric separation R and photon reception at t=0 under affine n,

\[
\Delta t_{GW-\gamma}=\frac R{c_0}-\frac{1-e^{-\gamma R/c_0}}\gamma.
\]

At an illustrative 40 Mpc, the delay is approximately 655,762 years, with the gravitational wave arriving later. At 30 and 50 Mpc it is approximately 369,176 and 1,023,770 years. GW170817 and its associated gamma-ray burst have a published gamma-ray-minus-GW delay of 1.74±0.05 seconds [4]. The distances here are benchmarks, not a rederived distance posterior in the new theory; source emission delays must be modeled. Nevertheless, a hundreds-of-thousands-of-years separation is a major conflict for the ordinary interpretation of the associated event. Matching the present light speed alone does not solve it.

A modified gravitational kinetic sector making tensor waves share the optical propagation history is a possible retained extension. Such a change also alters the gravitational field equations, the energy budget and matter couplings. It cannot be imposed as an isolated correction while keeping every Einstein-sector conclusion unchanged.

## 6. Fixed higher-redshift brightness protocol

The test uses the official Pantheon+SH0ES release [5,6], pinned to commit c447f0fea703fcd0fff57de5000947b5ca81286b. A protocol was written before inspecting the brightness outcomes; this is an internal frozen analysis, not an external preregistration. The coefficient κ=0.000077315 per million light-years and both laws S=exp(κR), DL=RS were held fixed.

For an isotropic source of fixed luminosity in Euclidean static space, conserved photons whose energies and arrival rates each fall by S give F=L/(4πR²S²). Thus

\[
D_L(z)=\frac{(1+z)\ln(1+z)}\kappa,
\qquad \mu=25+5\log_{10}[D_L/\mathrm{Mpc}].
\]

This functional distance law also occurs in coasting expansion models [7]; agreement with it would not uniquely identify a time mechanism. For the primary catalog calculation, R uses zHD and the outside factor uses 1+zHEL, following the released observer-frame convention. Transferring that convention to this theory is conditional. A zHD-only sensitivity check is reported below.

The release contains 1701 light-curve rows. The calibration uses only its 77 Cepheid-host rows representing 43 distinct supernovae. Generalized least squares on m_b_corr−CEPH_DIST, using their full released covariance, gives absolute magnitude M=−19.248885±0.030106. The calibrator statistic is χ²=57.135 for 76 degrees of freedom. No higher-redshift brightness is used to choose that calibration or κ.

The evaluation set contains 960 distinct noncalibrator supernovae with zHD≥0.1, spanning 0.10165–2.26137. Repeated calibrator light curves are retained with their released correlations; the selected higher-redshift rows happen to be unique objects. This is a new brightness evaluation in this project, but some objects may overlap earlier duration studies. It should not be described as an entirely independent sky sample.

Let Ccc, Chh and Chc be covariance blocks, and let w=Ccc⁻¹1/(1ᵀCcc⁻¹1). Then M̂=wᵀ(mcal−μCepheid), σM²=wᵀCccw, and q=Chcw. For residual r=mh−μmodel−M̂1, the covariance used is

\[
V=C_{hh}+\sigma_M^2\mathbf1\mathbf1^T-q\mathbf1^T-\mathbf1q^T.
\]

This propagates calibration uncertainty and shared systematic correlations without fitting the high-redshift data. The released matrix has a maximum antisymmetric difference of 3×10⁻⁸ mag²; it is symmetrized by (C+Cᵀ)/2 before Cholesky solves. The full covariance, not the per-row diagonal error column, is used throughout. A conditional-Gaussian calibration diagnostic is also included in the code.

## 7. Brightness outcomes

Positive residual means an observed standardized supernova is fainter than the unchanged model predicts.

| Redshift interval | Supernovae | Mean residual, mag | Correlated-GLS uncertainty, mag | Equivalent distance excess |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.10–0.30 | 466 | +0.1383 | 0.0314 | 6.6% |
| 0.30–0.60 | 365 | +0.2004 | 0.0323 | 9.7% |
| 0.60–1.00 | 104 | +0.2182 | 0.0380 | 10.6% |
| 1.00–2.26 | 25 | +0.3631 | 0.0708 | 18.2% |

The bins are correlated, including the common calibrator uncertainty. Their complete covariance is saved. The second minus first bin is +0.06208±0.01271 mag; the highest minus first bin is +0.22487±0.06460 mag. Testing the four bin means against a single constant residual gives χ²=36.743 for three degrees of freedom under the released covariance. This isolates a redshift-dependent discrepancy rather than treating four calibration-correlated offsets as independent measurements.

For a fixed reference only, use flat ΛCDM with Ωm=0.3 and H₀=c₀κ=75.5980 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹, without fitting either to this set. The comparison is:

| Statistic | Fixed temporal law | Fixed reference |
|---|---:|---:|
| χ², 960 residuals | 898.475 | 838.483 |
| Unweighted magnitude RMSE | 0.2421 | 0.1555 |
| Best constant residual offset, diagnostic only | +0.16059 | +0.04346 |
| χ² after subtracting that diagnostic offset, 959 dof | 871.552 | 836.512 |

The higher-redshift offset subtraction is explicitly a shape-only diagnostic, not a replacement prediction or refitted coefficient. The primary results retain the original law and calibration. The difference is about 60 in χ² for the fixed models and 35 after each is allowed one constant diagnostic offset. These are comparisons of specified models, not a complete cosmological evidence calculation. Neither model is optimized here; the reference's absolute residual includes the intentionally fixed scale.

The temporal model's total χ² is below its number of residuals. It would therefore be misleading to claim a catastrophic global goodness-of-fit rejection from that statistic alone. The evidence for a problem is the coherent bin trend and poorer relative fit under the supplied covariance. No extreme significance is assigned to the entire theory.

Using zHD for both redshift factors changes any predicted magnitude by at most 0.00253 mag; temporal χ² becomes 897.936. A conditional-Gaussian treatment of calibrator residual information gives χ²=907.720. Neither sensitivity check removes the main discrepancy. No additional selection, luminosity evolution, opacity, radiation-backreaction or age correction was fitted after inspecting the result.

## 8. What the real-data test does and does not establish

Pantheon+ provides standardized brightnesses, not unprocessed photons. SALT2 training, color and stretch corrections, host corrections, selection simulations, intrinsic scatter, peculiar velocities and parts of the covariance carry published assumptions. The release explicitly notes a fiducial ΛCDM model in its peculiar-velocity magnitude uncertainty [5]. The Cepheid calibration carries distance-ladder assumptions as well. These facts limit the independence of any exotic-theory comparison; they do not automatically explain away a trend.

The existing nearby fit's statistical coefficient uncertainty and distance-scale systematics were not propagated into the primary test because the requested coefficient was fixed. A future uncertainty-band analysis must label those effects separately, rather than use high-redshift data to move the coefficient unnoticed. A changed source-luminosity law would also need a consistent matter and stellar model. A changed photon survival probability would need a transport mechanism and spectrum/duration checks. Each would be an extension to the tested branch.

The shared-action calculation is therefore relevant to interpreting the brightness result: a mechanism that fails to preserve atomic and stellar behavior cannot simply borrow an unchanged standard-candle luminosity while claiming a fully derived common theory. The phenomenological brightness test remains useful, but it is conditional on the fixed-source assumptions and is not a validation of the incomplete action.

## 9. Candidate status and next discriminating work

| Candidate component | Progress | Specific unresolved issue |
|---|---|---|
| Covariant common action | Explicit gauge field, matter, propagation scalar, preferred-frame vector and gravity terms | Full perturbation stability and microscopic nuclear completion unproved |
| Fixed electrostatic binding | Obtained with Z=1/n | Magnetic hyperfine response remains, contradicting exact atomic invariance |
| Conservative propagation | Recovers the earlier ray cone and radiation source in the homogeneous limit | Backreaction changes the affine history |
| Static flat Einstein background | Field equations tested | Positive ρ+p prevents the intended static solution |
| Gravitational-wave propagation | Derived from the specified gravitational sector | Past cone mismatch produces an enormous benchmark delay |
| Fixed higher-z brightness law | Tested on 960 supernovae with full covariance and Cepheid-only calibration | Residual trend and worse comparison statistic; published processing assumptions remain |

The most useful theoretical next step is a common coupling that protects at least one optical/hyperfine ratio and aligns tensor-wave propagation while admitting a nonexpanding gravitational solution. Those requirements should be derived together, before fitting new astronomical parameters. A curved static branch and a modified gravitational kinetic sector remain possibilities, each with its own stability and distance predictions. More flexible couplings are not assumed to solve these problems merely because they introduce adjustable functions.

On the data side, the current result is already a substantive fixed-law issue. The next improvement is a forward light-curve and selection analysis, or an independent standardized-candle release with calibration fixed in advance. This would assess whether the redshift trend survives processing choices. Existing formulas, mismatches and comparison results should remain in the paper as the baseline against which extensions are judged.
